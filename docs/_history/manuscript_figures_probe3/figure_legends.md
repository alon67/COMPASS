# Figure legends

Legends for the six main-text figures built by
[`notebooks/COMPASS_figures.ipynb`](../notebooks/COMPASS_figures.ipynb). Every
number quoted below is regenerated by that notebook from the frozen tables in
`results/analyses/`; the values are written to
`results/figures/figure_values.csv` so a legend and its figure cannot drift
apart. The narrative record of the same tables is [`docs/RESULTS.md`](RESULTS.md);
§8 of this file lists the places where RESULTS.md prose predates the last table
regeneration and therefore quotes a different number.

**Conventions used throughout.** O-information is a polarity quantity and is
drawn on a diverging blue–red scale: blue, Ω < 0, synergy; red, Ω > 0,
redundancy. RSI has the opposite polarity by definition (RSI > 0 is synergy) and
is never plotted on the same axis as Ω. Categorical model colours are grouped by
source lineage — the two Hodgkin–Huxley implementations share blues, the two
Pospischil implementations share oranges — and each model additionally carries
its own marker, so colour is never the only channel; the palette was checked
under simulated deuteranopia and protanopia (minimum ΔE = 12.9 among the eight
model colours, ΔE ≈ 88 between the two polarity poles). Acceptance tolerances are
quoted as the maximum z-deviation from the model's own canonical 13-feature
phenotype that a sample may show and still be accepted (3.0, 2.0, 1.0, 0.5 z,
corresponding to ensembles E3, E3b, E4, E4b). Unless stated otherwise, panels use
the Gaussian estimator on absolute (non-rheobase-normalised) protocols with the
max-z acceptance metric — one row per model × regime. `mainen_l5pc` fails the N3
convergence gate and is excluded from primary inference; it is marked ‡ and
appears only in panels whose source table retains it, so the cohort differs
between panels of one figure and each panel states which it uses. All figures are
180 mm wide.

## Figure 1. A panel of eight independently implemented models, and the single manipulation applied to all of them.

**(A)** Membrane potential of each model in response to its canonical
suprathreshold current step, a 260 ms window beginning 10 ms before step onset,
traces offset vertically and ordered as in every other figure (Hodgkin–Huxley
pair, Pospischil pair, then the remaining four). The panel spans a
one-compartment Hodgkin–Huxley cell to a 2,304-compartment layer-5 pyramidal
reconstruction, 6 to 15 free parameters, eight implementations of six
independently developed source lineages, and source publications from 1952 to
2011. Parameters are multiplicative scale factors on each model's canonical
per-segment conductance profile, so a factor of two means the same thing in a
point model and in a reconstruction, and published spatial channel gradients are
preserved. **(B)** Panel scope: compartment count (log scale) against number of
free parameters, one point per model. Dashed lines join two implementations of
one source lineage (`hh_point`/`hh_cable`, which differ only in whether a
dendrite exists; `pospischil_rs`/`pospischil_fs`, two cell classes from one
framework, lab and mechanism set — these two coincide at one compartment).
**(C)** Accepted ensemble size against acceptance tolerance, one line per model.
Shading marks n < 250; at n = 250 the sign of net O-information still flips in
11% of resamples on average and in 35% for the worst ensemble (panel 3D), so the
band marks where the endpoint becomes unreliable rather than a threshold below
which it is and above which it is not. Coverage falls from 8 models at 3.0 z to 3
at 0.5 z, and the smallest accepted ensemble falls from 140 to 66 samples.
**(D)** The ensemble design. Every ensemble descends from one independent
quasi-random prior (E1) in which parameters are uncorrelated by construction. Two
re-sampled priors introduce dependence without conditioning (E2,
covariance-preserving; E6, clustered basins). The conditioning ladder E3 → E4b
accepts samples whose 13-feature phenotype lies within a tightening tolerance of
the model's own canonical values. Four controls are derived from the accepted
ensemble: a size-matched subset (E3m), a high-covariance subset (E7), a
column-shuffled null that destroys all dependence (E8) and a copula null (E9).
Campaign: 76,288 screening simulations plus 23,040 Saltelli simulations, with 0
failed simulations in the final tables.

## Figure 2. The estimator is validated against known structure, and the phenomenon reproduces in systems with no neurons in them.

**(A)** O-information for seven synthetic systems with known dependence
structure, computed at N = 16,000 by three estimators (solid, Gaussian; hatched,
copula; dotted, k-nearest-neighbour). The ground-truth sign is printed under each
system name; for the independent system, where all three bars are sub-pixel, the
three values are printed instead. All three estimators recover every redundancy
case and the sum-constrained synergy case (Ω = −3.27). The Gaussian estimator and
its rank-transformed copula variant return **0.000** where synergy is carried by
a product — |Ω| < 2 × 10⁻⁶, i.e. indistinguishable from zero at this sample size —
while the k-NN estimator returns −0.839 (product of two) and −1.566 (product of
three). This is a property of the instrument, and it bounds every Gaussian
O-information result in this study and in the literature it addresses: absence of
Gaussian O-information is not absence of higher-order structure. Pooling two
separately-fitted populations, each with internally independent variables, is
scored as strong redundancy (+0.30 to +0.49 nats) by every estimator. **(B)**
Redundancy–synergy index validated on three analytic cases: a sum of independent
inputs (+1.97, synergy), duplicated predictors (−1.82, redundancy) and a single
informative predictor (0.00). RSI polarity is opposite to that of Ω. **(C, D)**
The identical conditioning workflow applied to two non-neuronal nonlinear systems
with six parameters each, both starting from an independent log-uniform prior of
20,000 samples. In the Elowitz–Leibler repressilator conditioned on an
oscillation phenotype, mean absolute pairwise correlation rises from 0.009 to
0.204 and O-information falls monotonically from 0.0000 to −0.183 nats as the
tolerance tightens, with the effective rank falling from 6.00 to 5.06 (not
plotted). In a three-species Lotka–Volterra community conditioned on coexistence,
correlation rises only slightly (0.009 → 0.030) and |Ω| never exceeds 0.0004,
because an asymptotic coexistence phenotype barely constrains the parameters. The
effect is therefore not automatic: it requires the phenotype to actually
constrain parameter space, which is a property of the map rather than of having
run the conditioning machinery.

**Claim level: Observed / Statistical.** These are facts about estimators and
about conditioned ensembles of nonlinear models, not about neurons.

## Figure 3. Conditioning an exactly independent prior on a shared phenotype manufactures higher-order dependence.

**(A)** Mean absolute pairwise Pearson correlation among accepted parameters, one
line per model, from the unconditioned prior (0.0017 averaged over the eight
models, i.e. independent by construction) through the four acceptance tolerances.
Every model gains correlation at every tolerance. Panel means are 0.052 at 3.0 z
and 0.291 at 0.5 z, but the panel composition changes with tolerance (8, 8, 6 and
3 models), so the per-model lines rather than the means carry the result.
**(B)** Change in net O-information relative to each model's own unconditioned
prior, on a symmetric-log axis, for the **primary cohort** (7 implementations, 5
source lineages; `mainen_l5pc` is absent from `conditioning_shift.parquet`).
Filled markers mark shifts that exceed that model's own conditional bootstrap
noise by ≥ 2×; open markers mark shifts that do not. All 7 implementations — 5 of
5 source lineages — move toward synergy at the permissive tolerance. **(C)** The
same shifts expressed as |Δ| divided by the model's own conditional bootstrap
standard deviation, obtained by resampling that model's accepted ensemble at its
own size (40 replicates). This panel uses `estimator_reliability.json`, which
retains all 8 implementations, so its denominators are 8 where panel B shows 7.
Counts above each column give the number of implementations whose shift is
resolvable at that tolerance: 5/8 → 6/8 → 2/6 → 1/3. Where the effect is
resolvable it is monotone in tolerance — `hh_cable` runs 4.9× → 7.5× → 8.9× →
5.3× its own noise with magnitude rising 16-fold — and every apparent sign
reversal sits at ≤ 2.0×. These are conditional bootstrap signal-to-noise ratios,
**not** significance levels: they resample only the already-selected accepted
parameters, hold the prior endpoint fixed, and do not repeat conditioning, target
estimation, or tuple and surrogate selection. **(D)** Why the reversals are
noise. Ensembles large enough to trust were subsampled down to each size (60
resamples per size, five reference ensembles) and the sign of the endpoint
recorded: at n = 50 the statistic takes the wrong sign in 23% of resamples on
average and in up to 48% for the worst ensemble, falling to 11% (mean) at
n = 250. The panel-level dose–response curve is therefore **not estimable**,
because panel composition changes with tolerance and resolution collapses as
acceptance falls; what is established is the direction of the effect and its
monotonicity in the models with the samples to see it.

**Claim level: Statistical.**

## Figure 4. The statistic usually reported is not a statistic.

The quantity this literature reports — the fraction of parameter tuples with
Ω < 0 — is compared with a family-wise surrogate distribution built from
column-shuffled ensembles evaluated over the *same number of tuples*, so that the
null reproduces the search rather than only the data. Panels use the Gaussian
estimator pooled over protocol family, acceptance metric and tuple order, which
is the slice RESULTS.md §7 reports. **(A)** Naive fraction of tuples with Ω < 0
against tuple order for the unconditioned prior (E1), the phenotype-conditioned
ensemble (E3) and the column-shuffled null (E8); thin lines are individual
models, bold lines the panel mean, series labelled directly. Seven of the eight
models have exactly 0.0% naive synergy in E1 at every order, so the E1 panel mean
(2.3–5.2%) is carried by `khaliq_purkinje` alone. **(B)** The same quantity after
surrogate control. **(C)** The headline contraction. In E8, where all dependence
has been deliberately destroyed, the naive statistic reports **45.5%**
"synergy-dominated" tuples; surrogate control returns **0.04%** — exactly zero in
7 of the 8 models, with one tuple of 56 exceeding the band in the eighth. In the
conditioned ensemble the naive 61.7% falls to 26.4%, so roughly half of a
reported "N% of tuples were synergy-dominated" figure is recoverable from noise.
**(D)** Per model in the shuffled null: naive (pale) against surrogate-controlled
(solid, value printed above each bar). The naive statistic reports 39–54%
synergy-dominated tuples in every model of an ensemble that contains no
dependence at all.

**Any report of "N% of tuples were synergy-dominated" without a matched-search
surrogate control is uninterpretable. Claim level: Statistical.**

## Figure 5. The sampling regime, not the neuron, sets the sign — and target-specific information moves the other way.

**(A)** Net O-information for every model × ensemble regime combination in the
endpoint slice, on a symmetric-log diverging scale (blue, synergy; red,
redundancy; dashes, not computed). Columns are internally consistent and differ
from one another; rows are not. The regime, not the model, organises the map.
**(B)** Mean standard deviation of net O-information within one model across
regimes (0.0052 nats) against within one regime across models (0.0029 nats): the
regime effect is **1.82×** the model effect. The grid is unbalanced — 7–11
regimes per model, 3–8 models per regime — so this ratio is a summary of the map
in **A** rather than a variance decomposition; it ranges 1.4–2.6 under
leave-one-regime-out and 1.5–2.4 under leave-one-model-out. The variance
decomposition is **C**. **(C)** Commonality analysis over predictor blocks, n =
166 endpoint rows. Bars give each block's unique (semi-partial) R², the variance
it explains that no other block can, for two endpoints. For synergy area,
sampling contributes 0.160 and geometry 0.025, while model identity and
architecture (parameter count, compartment count, point-model flag) each
contribute **exactly 0.000** — identity falls below the 1 × 10⁻⁴ resolution
floor, so the sampling-and-geometry-over-identity ratio is a lower bound rather
than a point estimate. Because the 166 rows are derived from one shared campaign,
the ratio is re-estimated by bootstrap over models with every model subsampled to
a common N per regime: median 13.1, 95% CI [4.0, 81.7], exceeding the
preregistered threshold of 2.0 in 100% of resamples. The interval is wide because
seven models is a small bootstrap unit, not because the effect is fragile.
**(D)** Leave-one-**model**-out prediction of synergy area, all seven folds shown
rather than the median alone, on a symmetric-log axis (stems, not bars, because
length on a symmetric-log axis is not proportional to value). Sampling and
geometry predict a held-out model at median R² = 0.729; architecture alone
predicts nothing and is often catastrophically negative (−88 for `hh_cable`). The
one failure of transfer, `migliore_ca1` (R² = −3.46), is the smallest ensemble in
the panel. *`lomo.parquet` also stores rows tagged `fold_level =
"source_lineage_id"`, but they are byte-identical to the `model_id` rows — seven
folds, one held-out model each — so the folds are per model, and the panel is
labelled accordingly. A genuine leave-one-lineage-out design would give five
folds and is not in this release.* **(E)** O-information and RSI respond to the
same conditioning in opposite directions, computed on the five models present at
every level so that a changing panel cannot produce the divergence. The fraction
of order-3 tuples with Ω < 0 rises from 3.7% in the unconditioned independent
prior to 58.4% at 1.0 z, while the fraction of tuples with RSI > 0 —
target-specific synergy about an electrical feature — is already **82.2%** in
that same prior, where parameters are uncorrelated by construction and no
conditioning artefact is available, and falls monotonically under conditioning to
**68.8%**. RSI rows are restricted to the max-z acceptance metric and averaged
within model before averaging across models, because `rsi_screening.parquet`
carries both protocol families and both acceptance metrics as separate rows.
Dependence among sampled parameters and target-specific information are different
objects.

A mixed model with model family as a random effect gives an adjusted intraclass
correlation of 0.161 and a variance-partition coefficient of 0.027 for model
identity. Both are reported as **descriptive only** and do not vote in the
preregistered decision, because the fixed covariates conditioned on — sampling
regime, geometry, sample size — are themselves the generic hypothesis.

**Claim level: Statistical.**

## Figure 6. Statistically synergistic tuples show more measured non-additivity than matched and random controls, but the support is imprecise, readout-dependent, and does not meet the preregistered validation criterion.

The six most synergistic order-3 tuples in each model's conditioned ensemble were
perturbed singly and jointly at eight background points drawn by k-means from the
accepted ensemble itself, with every displacement kept inside the declared prior.
Non-additivity is η = [f(x₀+Σδᵢ) − f(x₀)] − Σᵢ[f(x₀+δᵢ) − f(x₀)], normalised by
the standard deviation of the population being probed. Two control families of
the same size are run identically: a **matched** set drawn from the middle of the
O-information distribution and a **uniformly random** set. The battery covers the
7 primary-cohort models (42 tuples per class); `mainen_l5pc` was not regenerated.

**(A)** |η| for every tuple, primary readout (mean firing rate). Filled points
are individual tuples, open points at the axis floor are the 16 tuples with
η = 0 exactly, black bars are class medians, and the class mean is printed above.
Top-synergy tuples reach mean |η| = 0.306 ensemble SD against 0.103 (matched) and
0.065 (random). The dashed line is |η| = 0.25 SD, which is **one of two**
conditions in the preregistered functional-support rule; the second is a sign
consistency ≥ 0.75 across the eight backgrounds, which is not visible in the
panel. 42.9% of top tuples lie above the line, but only **31.0%** satisfy both
conditions, against 7.1% (matched) and 2.4% (random). The preregistered H-B
criterion requires **≥ 50%** of top tuples to be functionally supported;
at 0.310 this criterion is **not met**, and it is the one H-B criterion the study
fails. **(B)** The same comparison within each model, matched control against top
synergy: 6 of 7 models increase. The panel mean is dominated by two models —
top-synergy mean |η| runs from 0.786 (`hh_cable`) and 0.709 (`hh_point`) down to
0.029 (`khaliq_purkinje`), a 27-fold spread. **(C)** Enrichment in mean |η| with
95% confidence intervals from a bootstrap over models: **4.74× [1.90, 9.65]**
against random controls (Wilcoxon p = 0.016, 7/7 models) and **2.97× [1.35,
7.71]** against matched controls (p = 0.047, 6/7). Both point estimates clear the
preregistered 1.5× threshold and both intervals exclude 1, but the upper bounds
are far from the point estimates — seven models is a small bootstrap unit, and
those seven represent five source lineages. The analysis code labels both
comparisons *inconclusive*; the quantity actually scored in the decision table is
the lineage-clustered paired difference, +0.203 SD, 95% CI [+0.018, +0.439] over
five lineages, not the point ratio. **(D)** The readout decides. Pearson
correlation between a tuple's O-information and its measured non-additivity,
computed over all 126 tuples of the three classes pooled, separately on each of
four features extracted from the *same* simulations with the *same* tuples and
the *same* parameter displacements; enrichment over matched controls is printed
at the right. The relationship is present for the two spike-timing readouts
(r = −0.52 for firing rate, −0.32 for first ISI; 2.97× and 2.79× enrichment) and
absent for the two waveform-shape readouts (r = −0.16 and −0.005; 1.32× and
0.85×, the latter running slightly the wrong way). Because Ω is bimodal across
tuple classes, Pearson r partly measures class membership; rank correlation gives
−0.36, −0.35, −0.02 and +0.01, which preserves the timing/waveform split and
weakens the AP-amplitude value further. "These parameters form a functional
module" is not a complete statement; the complete statement names the readout.
**(E)** Precision at the level of individual tuples. Each of the 42 top-synergy
tuples is shown as its |mean η| with the 95% percentile-bootstrap interval over
its eight backgrounds, ranked; intervals that straddle zero are drawn to the axis
floor. Only **16 of 42** intervals exclude zero; the rest are indistinguishable
from no interaction. **(F)** The parallel variance-based result. Sobol
interaction fraction 1 − ΣS₁ per model and feature, computed only on the
unconditioned Saltelli design. Filled points are the 8 of 56 analyses that pass
the quotability gate — complete (valid fraction ≥ 0.90), precise (widest S1
confidence half-width at most half the largest S1 it describes) and physically
plausible (interaction fraction in [0,1], |mean(ST − S1)| ≤ 1); crosses are the
47 that fail it and are plotted, triangles the 2 of those clipped at the axis
limit (largest 2.67), and one further analysis has no estimate at all. Shading
marks values outside [0,1], which are estimator noise rather than interaction
mass. The mean interaction fraction is 0.148 over the quotable analyses against
0.569 ungated. This is not a finding that interactions are weak; it is a finding
that at these base sample sizes most Sobol estimates cannot support a
quantitative statement in either direction.

A companion re-run of the whole battery at half the campaign time step was used
to ask whether these η values survive a finer integration. The prespecified
repeatability rule (median |Δη| ≤ 0.05 SD **and** ≥ 80% of tuple means keeping
their sign) is met by only **2 of the 5** re-run models, so the gate is not
passed. Where the instability sits is nonetheless informative and is measured
rather than asserted: 17 of 32 tuples below 0.02 SD flip sign, 2 of 32 in the
0.02–0.1 SD band do, and **none of the 26 tuples at or above 0.10 SD** flips.

**Claim level: Functional.** That the perturbations produce the η values reported
is a measurement and stands. That those η values *validate* the tuples
statistical synergy picked out is an inference, and on this evidence it is
suggestive rather than established: the preregistered support criterion is not
met, the enrichment intervals span an order of magnitude and the analysis code
calls both comparisons inconclusive, most individual tuples are not
distinguishable from no interaction, the numerical-repeatability gate is not
passed, and the support is a property of a spike-timing measurement rather than
of the parameters alone. No claim at the **Biological** level is made anywhere in
this work; that would require paired molecular and physiological measurement in
the same cells.
