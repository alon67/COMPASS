# COMPASS results

**Are the higher-order parameter interactions reported in phenotype-constrained
neuronal models neuron-specific biological signatures, or generic consequences of
nonlinear model structure, parameter sampling, covariance, and conditioning on
selected outputs?**

Every figure and number below is regenerated by
[`notebooks/COMPASS_results.ipynb`](../notebooks/COMPASS_results.ipynb) from the
frozen tables in `results/analyses/`. Hypotheses, endpoints and decision
thresholds were fixed in [`docs/preregistration.md`](preregistration.md) before
the screening campaign was analysed.

Claims are separated throughout into the four levels of blueprint §30 —
**Observed**, **Statistical**, **Functional**, **Biological** — and the fourth is
never asserted.

re-read the tree after the fixes in §0.0. It confirmed those repairs and found
four further problems that change what the data mean, plus a set of provenance
and release defects. **The largest of them is a measurement defect that changed
stored simulation results**, so this section leads with that rather than with the
release machinery.

| what changed | why | consequence |
|---|---|---|

Release-machinery corrections of record:

* **The release manifest covered 185 files.** It hashed whatever JSON and
  Parquet sat under three result directories and omitted the feature shards, the
  archived traces, the figures, the reports, most of `data/provenance`, the model
  assets and the source tree. There is now a **declared inventory**

  the release rather than passing silently.
* **`run_all.sh --force` was parsed and never used.** It now has a definition
  (quarantine derived artifacts; `--force-simulations` also quarantines the

### 0.0.2 The 2026-08-09 independent post-response-3 audit

A sixth review
(`COMPASS_INDEPENDENT_POST_RESPONSE_3_TECHNICAL_SCIENTIFIC_PUBLICATION_MATURITY_AUDIT_2026-08-09.md`)
read the tree after §0.0.1 and returned **major revision; not submission-ready**,
with seven publication-blocking findings. Its closing instruction was not to
repair another analysis on the stored corpus but to reset the measurement and
regenerate from it. **That is what this section records.**

The finding that forced it: `find_rheobase` was still a **sparse search
presented as an exhaustive one**. §0.0.1 replaced a single ceiling probe with a
geometric ladder (`hi/2**k`, k = 8…1) and said firing was no longer assumed
monotonic. Eight probes over two decades is not a search of an interval. The
reviewer supplied a deterministic counterexample — a cell firing from 0.30 to
0.40 nA under a 1.0 nA ceiling, straddled by the 0.25 and 0.50 nA rungs — for
which the function returned `None`. Worse, the ladder ran **only when the
ceiling did not spike**, so a cell with a low disconnected window *and* an upper
firing branch was bisected on the upper branch: its rheobase was not missed, it
was overstated.

#### What replaced it

`(lo, hi]` is divided into `RHEOBASE_SCAN_POINTS = 64` equal steps of width
`delta = (hi - lo)/64`. `lo` is probed, the ceiling is probed and recorded, and
the grid is scanned **upward from the bottom on every sample** until the first
amplitude that spikes; bisection then refines inside that one bracket. The
guarantee is a string in the source, quoted into the release manifest:

> **GUARANTEED:** every firing window of width >= delta lying in [lo, hi] is
> detected, whether or not the ceiling spikes and whether or not firing is
> monotonic in current. **NOT GUARANTEED:** firing windows narrower than delta.
> `None` therefore means *no spike at any probed amplitude at this resolution*,
> never *silent at every real-valued amplitude*.

No finite search can promise more, and the previous docstring promised more.

#### The corpus was regenerated, not repaired

`measurement_schema` is part of every shard's reuse identity, so moving it to
`probe-4` invalidated all 4,656 shards and the campaign runners re-simulated
from the immutable parameter draws. **130,098 rows, six campaigns, 25 h 06 min,
zero simulation errors.** Every campaign reproduces its previous row count
exactly — no draw lost, none duplicated.

`tools/repair_rheobase.py` was **deleted**. In-place mutation of a stored shard
is no longer an operation this repository offers, and no enumeration argument is
made anywhere: under a genuinely non-monotonic model a ceiling-spiking row can
have a lower window, so *no* row is unchanged by construction.

The rheobase delta is flat at zero through the 1st and 99th percentiles: this
moved a handful of rows a long way (min −1.163 nA, one whole firing window below
what bisection reached) rather than every row a little. Changes concentrate in
two broad-prior models, `khaliq_purkinje` (486) and `dougalis_vlpag_da` (330).

**The 25 rows that got worse are the contract's admitted limit, measured.**
probe-3's bisection could stumble into a window narrower than `delta`; the grid
steps over it. That is 0.019% of the corpus, against 250 rows corrected the
other way — and it is reported rather than netted off.

#### Is 64 points enough? Measured

[`rheobase_resolution_audit.json`](../data/provenance/rheobase_resolution_audit.json)
re-scans a stratified sample at **4x the campaign grid — 256 points,
exhaustively**, recording every transition instead of stopping at the first.
37 of 37 draws succeeded:

* **0** windows narrower than the campaign grid step;
* **0** draws whose lowest window the campaign grid would have missed;
* **0** draws with disconnected firing windows;
* narrowest window found anywhere = **9.8x** the grid step, median **58.9x**;
* **14 of 37** draws silent at 4x resolution too — a `NaN` that survives being
  looked at four times harder.

The grid is not marginally adequate; it is about an order of magnitude finer
than the narrowest real feature. Zero disconnected windows does **not** mean
f–I curves are monotonic — 86 rows were rescued from block at the top of the
range — it means no cell in this sample had two *separated* firing regions.

#### One voltage band, and what it cost

The corpus excluded traces outside `[-160, 120] mV` while the waveform battery
flagged outside `[-150, 80] mV`, so a trace reaching −155 mV was
"physiologically impossible" in the report and fully analysis-eligible in the
inference. The audit required every invalid class to be excluded by a rule
propagated to every downstream analysis; two bands cannot do that. There is now
one, `PHYSICAL_V_MIN_MV`/`PHYSICAL_V_MAX_MV`, set at the tighter pair.

**Cost: 609 rows leave the analysis set and none enter** — 397
`khaliq_purkinje`, 212 `dougalis_vlpag_da`, across four campaigns. The
violations are on the hyperpolarised side (AHP depths reaching −200 mV under
negative current), not at the spike peak.

Choosing the *tighter* band is a judgement, not something the audit specified:
it required one band, not which one. The tighter one is chosen because the
project's own comment calls that range a broken-simulation detector, and
unifying at the looser end would declare analysis-eligible the very traces the
waveform gate calls impossible. Because it is a judgement, it is **measured
rather than asserted**: all 609 keys are recorded in
[`voltage_band_tightened_rows.json`](../data/provenance/voltage_band_tightened_rows.json),
and the `wide_voltage_band` sensitivity arm re-admits the **251** of them that
are in the discovery campaign — every one `khaliq_purkinje` — reporting the
count it actually restored in
[`voltage_band_arm.json`](../data/provenance/voltage_band_arm.json) rather than
the size of the key file. The remaining 358 are in campaigns this arm does not
read (`screening_v2_replication` 212, `sobol` 81, `screening_scramble2` 65).

**The result: the band choice is not load-bearing.** The arm returns
**H-G 5/5, H-B 0/2 — the same verdict as `primary`**, on the same 370 ensemble
rows over the same 5 source lineages (the re-admitted rows join existing
ensembles rather than adding new ones). Three H-G criterion values move and
none crosses its threshold: the unique-R² ratio 1651.4 → 1662.1, the held-out
lineage R² 0.4988 → 0.4889, and the regime classifier 0.2928 → 0.3031. Both
H-B criteria are unchanged, because the models carrying them are not the models
whose rows moved.

Re-admission strips the `nonphysical_voltage` flag, which a row stores
*protocol-qualified* — `hyperpol_abs:nonphysical_voltage`. The first
implementation compared whole tokens against the bare name, matched nothing and
stripped nothing, and the unit test written alongside it used bare-name
fixtures, so the test agreed with the bug. What caught it was the function's own
post-condition, which re-checks with a substring and refused to return. The arm
now fails closed twice over: on a strip that does not apply, and on a run that
re-admits no row at all.

#### What the regeneration changed in the result

At the level of the **decision**, nothing. All five H-G criteria return the same
verdict; the effective sample is identical row for row (370 ensemble rows, 332
deduplicated, 7 implementations, 5 source lineages, 6 neuron classes, 0 classes
with two independent lineages). Of the 410 primary-arm endpoint rows, **356
(86.8%) are identical to the bit**, none was added and none dropped; the largest
movement in `synergy_area` is 0.28 between-row SD and the median movement is
zero. The noisiest endpoint is `intrinsic_dimension` at 0.82 SD, which is the
one most sensitive to which rows are in an ensemble, and 609 left.

At the level of **H-B, the change is real and it is an improvement in
auditability rather than in evidence.** Under probe-3 both H-B criteria were
*withheld*: the stored battery recorded no measurement schema and its inputs had
been regenerated beneath it (§0.0.1). They are now evaluable, and both **fail**:

| | probe-3 (withheld) | probe-4 (scored) |
|---|---:|---:|
| functional support, top vs matched control | 0.167 vs 0.111 | **0.167 vs 0.167** |
| mean \|η\|, top vs matched control | 0.129 vs 0.119 SD | 0.151 vs 0.153 SD |
| ratio | 1.09x | **0.99x** |
| paired difference, lineage-clustered | — | −0.002 SD, 95% CI [−0.023, +0.037] |

**4 of the 8 implementations in the battery are numerically repeatable** at
half their campaign time step — `khaliq_purkinje`, `mainen_l5pc`,
`migliore_ca1`, `pospischil_rs` — and the primary arm excludes `mainen_l5pc`
under gate N3, leaving **three implementations spanning three lineages** to
carry the H-B criteria. That half the panel fails a repeatability check at half
dt is a first-order limitation of the battery and is stated here rather than
left in the JSON: the criteria are scored on the subset that survives it, which
is the minimum the precondition accepts.

On the three repeatable lineages that carry the criteria, **statistically
synergistic tuples are indistinguishable from matched middle-Ω controls.** The
18 top-synergy tuples are the *same 18* under both corpora, so this is not a
change of which modules were selected. H-B stays **UNRESOLVED** — a failure to
demonstrate is not a demonstration of absence — but it is now unresolved against
a measured null instead of against a missing input.

The whole-battery contrast, which is descriptive and not scored, falls from
**2.49x to 1.43x**, and the reason decomposes cleanly:

* `mainen_1996` **entered the battery**: under probe-3 it had no accepted
  ensemble large enough to draw backgrounds from, under probe-4 it does. It is
  the one lineage where top-synergy tuples are *less* non-additive than their
  controls (0.047 vs 0.113). Removing it recovers 1.62x. It is excluded from
  the primary arm by gate N3 and therefore casts no vote in the scored
  criteria; `retain_mainen` measures what it would do (0.88x over 4 lineages).
* Of the five lineages present under both, **three are numerically identical**
  (`hay_2011`, `migliore_1999`, `pospischil_2008`) and two moved — the two with
  the largest corpus changes. `hodgkin_huxley_1952`'s top-tuple non-additivity
  halved (0.747 to 0.377); `khaliq_2003`'s *control* rose ninefold (0.012 to
  0.116), which is what flattens the scored ratio.

That `khaliq_purkinje` and the HH lineage are exactly where the corpus moved —
397 of the 609 band exclusions and the largest share of the 275 rheobase
corrections — is the expected coupling, not a coincidence, and it is the
clearest single illustration of why the audit refused a targeted repair.

adapted, compiled and audited. That is expensive and it is the point: a gate
that never rejects anything that cost something is not doing any work.

* **Neuron identity vs laboratory.** `mainen_l5pc` and `bbp_l5pc` are the same

* **Cell class vs implementation.** `pospischil_rs` and `pospischil_fs` are
  different cell classes from *one* framework, lab and mechanism set.
* **Morphology vs channels.** `hh_point` and `hh_cable` have identical channel

* **Lineage vs class.** The dopaminergic class now contains two independently

  three independent lineages the audit requires before a class-specific claim,
  and none is made.**

### 2.1 Parameterisation

Parameters are **multiplicative scale factors on each model's canonical
per-segment conductance profile**, sampled log-uniformly with the published model
at scale 1.0. Two consequences matter scientifically:

1. A factor of two means the same thing in a one-compartment point model and in a
   1,106-compartment reconstruction, which is what makes cross-model comparison
   defensible (blueprint §14.2 forbids comparing raw magnitudes).
2. Published spatial channel gradients are preserved — Hay's apical `Ih` gradient
   and dendritic Ca²⁺ hot zone, Mainen's axonal Na⁺ hotspot, Migliore's
   distance-dependent A-current. Flattening them would silently confound "neuron
   identity" with "we deleted the channel gradient".

**Evidence tiers.** `bbp_l5pc` uses bounds derived from Blue Brain's own published
optimisation configuration (`BluePyOpt/examples/l5pc/config/params.json`) —
**tier C, a model-fitting prior**. All other models use expert-defined ranges —
**tier D, a sensitivity range**. *No parameter in this study is tier A or B.*
Nothing here may therefore be described as measured biological variability.

All eight models reproduce their published behaviour
([`model_audit.md`](model_audits/model_audit.md)):

| model | published | reproduced |

| `pospischil_rs` | V_rest −70 mV, R_in 34 MΩ (source comment) | −70.5 mV, 34.2 MΩ |
| `pospischil_fs` | V_rest −70 mV, R_in 48 MΩ (source comment) | −70.0 mV, 47.3 MΩ |
| `khaliq_purkinje` | spontaneous ~30–50 Hz at 22 °C | 30.0 Hz, spontaneous |

Two reproduction issues were found and are reported rather than papered over:

* **Migliore's initial-state procedure.** The published model pins its resting
  potential by solving `e_pas(x) = v(x) + (ina(x)+ik(x))/g_pas(x)` after
  `finitialize`. Omitting this leaves the cell at −74.9 mV instead of −65 mV.
  It is reproduced and re-run **for every parameter sample**, because the
  standing currents depend on the conductances being sampled.
* **Migliore's segmentation.** Its as-distributed mesh (559 segments) differs by
  one spike from finer meshes on a knife-edge 0.5 nA step. It runs on the
  d_lambda(100 Hz) mesh (1106 segments), which the feature-level audit accepts
  with agreement 1.00 on all 13 features (§5.1).
* **Mainen's segmentation is not converged, and neither is its replacement.**
  The published "no compartment longer than 50 um" rule (754 segments) fails the
  feature-level audit at every time step. It runs on twice the d_lambda mesh
  (2304 segments) at dt=0.0125 ms, which is a large improvement but still fails
  one conditioning feature against a reference refined in both time and space
  (§5.1.1). Treat Mainen's absolute `adaptation_index` as unreliable.

### 2.3 Numerical policy

The campaign runs **deterministic fixed time steps**, each the largest the
*feature-level* convergence audit accepted for that model (§5.1) — not CVODE, even though CVODE is
faster at the canonical point. Under a broad prior CVODE's cost depends on how
stiff each individual sample happens to be: profiling `mainen_l5pc` over six
prior samples gave a median of 11 s per sample but individual samples of 233 s
and 307 s. That distribution cannot be budgeted, and abandoning slow samples
would bias the ensemble toward the well-behaved region of parameter space.

## 3. Estimator validation

No neuronal number is believed until the estimators are shown to recover known
structure. Sign conventions are asserted against analytic discrete systems in
`tests/numerical/test_information_benchmarks.py` (31 tests), and against
continuous systems in `results/analyses/benchmarks.json`.

Frozen conventions:

* **O-information** Ω > 0 → redundancy, Ω < 0 → synergy.
* **RSI** = I(X₁…Xₙ;Y) − Σᵢ I(Xᵢ;Y): > 0 → synergy, < 0 → redundancy.

Note the *opposite* polarity of the two. All three RSI sign cases validate:
sum-of-independent-inputs → +1.97 (synergy), duplicated predictors → −1.82
(redundancy), single informative predictor → 0.0000.

### Finding E1 — the Gaussian and copula estimators are blind to product-type synergy

O-information at N = 16,000, four variables (Figure 2):

| system | truth | Gaussian | copula | k-NN |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| independent | none | 0.000 | 0.000 | 0.003 |
| duplicated | redundancy | **+1.151** | **+1.150** | **+1.162** |
| linear correlated | redundancy | **+0.268** | **+0.268** | **+0.270** |
| mixture (pooled populations) | redundancy | **+0.386** | **+0.301** | **+0.489** |
| sum-constrained | synergy | **−3.274** | **−3.247** | **−2.797** |
| product of two inputs | synergy | 0.000 | 0.000 | **−0.839** |
| product of three inputs | synergy | 0.000 | 0.000 | **−1.566** |

The covariance-based estimator used throughout this literature — and its
rank-based copula variant — return **exactly zero** for synergy carried by a
product, where a k-NN estimator returns −0.84 and −1.57 nats. Both detect
synergy expressed in second-order statistics (the sum constraint). This is a
property of the instrument, and it bounds what any Gaussian O-information result,
including every neuronal result below, can claim: *absence of Gaussian
O-information is not absence of higher-order structure*.

**Observed / Statistical.** A methodological result about estimators, not neurons.

### Finding E2 — the O-information estimator is unbiased across tuple orders, unlike total correlation

Writing cᵢ = ψ((N−i)/2) − log((N−1)/2) ≈ −i/(N−1), the small-sample bias entering
O-information is −2Σ_{i<n} cᵢ + (n−2)cₙ + n·c₁ ≈ [n(n−1) − n² + 2n − n]/(N−1) = 0.
The leading term cancels identically. Empirically, on independent data with
N = 300, mean Ω stays below 0.001 nats at every order from 3 to 12, while total
correlation is biased by +0.006 to +0.110 nats over the same range.

Order curves of Ω can therefore be compared directly across orders; order curves
of TC cannot. Estimator *variance* still grows with order, which is why every
result below carries a per-order surrogate control.

### Finding E3 — pooling separately-fitted populations manufactures redundancy

The `mixture_pooled` benchmark contains two well-separated clusters, each with
internally independent variables. Every estimator reports strong redundancy
(+0.30 to +0.49 nats) even though there is no dependence *within* either
population. Any ensemble assembled by pooling separately-fitted cell models
inherits this artefact.

## 4. Non-neuronal comparators

The same conditioning and O-information workflow, applied to two nonlinear
dynamical systems with comparable parameter counts and no neurons in them
(blueprint §17.3): the Elowitz–Leibler repressilator (6 parameters, oscillation
phenotype) and a three-species Lotka–Volterra community (6 parameters,
coexistence phenotype). Both start from an independent log-uniform prior of
20,000 samples and are conditioned on their own canonical phenotype using the
identical machinery applied to the neuron models.

**Repressilator** (`results/analyses/benchmarks.json`):

| ensemble | n | acceptance | Ω (nats) | mean \|r\| | effective rank |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| unconditioned prior | 20,000 | — | **+0.0000** | 0.009 | 5.998 |
| conditioned, permissive | 12,730 | 64% | −0.0018 | 0.055 | 5.923 |
| conditioned, moderate | 10,591 | 53% | −0.0112 | 0.073 | 5.817 |
| conditioned, strict | 4,083 | 20% | −0.0681 | 0.168 | 5.325 |
| conditioned, very strict | 789 | 3.9% | **−0.1826** | 0.204 | 5.062 |

Conditioning an *exactly independent* prior on a shared phenotype drives
parameter correlation from 0.009 to 0.204 and O-information from 0.0000 to
**−0.183 nats**, monotonically across all four tolerances, with the effective
rank falling from 6.00 to 5.06 as the accepted set collapses onto a lower-
dimensional sheet. **The effect appears in full in a gene circuit with no
neurons in it.**

**Lotka–Volterra** shows a much weaker correlation response (0.009 → 0.030) and

a negative result: **the effect is not automatic.** It requires the phenotype to
actually constrain the parameters, which is a property of the map, not of having
run the conditioning machinery.

**Observed / Statistical.** These are facts about conditioned ensembles of
nonlinear models. They establish that finding the same pattern in a neuron model
is not, by itself, evidence about neurons.

### What the gates found

Building the gates was not a formality. Each of the following was found by a
gate, not by inspection, and each cost something to fix:

1. **425 `pospischil_rs` E1 rows came from a different Sobol design.** Sample ids
   925-1349 were simulated under seed 20262103 while the other 7,767 used
   20262247, so the stored E1 table was a mixture of two scrambles. Detected the
   first time identity-validated reuse ran; the 17 shards were quarantined and

   three spikes; the stored rows are enumerated as a documented, non-load-bearing
   drift rather than hidden, and `tools/validate_campaign.py` **re-derives** the
   "non-load-bearing" claim from the analysis code, so the allowance evaporates
   if `cv_isi` ever becomes a conditioning feature.
3. **`dougalis_vlpag_da` had an unmeasurable hyperpolarising step.** The frozen
   amplitude rule (-0.5 x rheobase) gave -0.002 nA for a cell whose rheobase is
   0.0039 nA; at that current the membrane *depolarises* by +2.8 mV over the
   step as the baseline settles, and input resistance -- one of the 13
   conditioning features -- comes out at **-223 MOhm**. Gate N6's sign check
   caught it. The rule now has a floor at -0.1 x the `hi` amplitude, which binds
   only for the two cells whose rheobase is at or near zero and changes nothing
   for the other twelve. The affected shards were quarantined on their
   `protocol_hash` and regenerated -- automatically, by the same mechanism.
4. **Project and eFEL spike counts differ on the two spontaneously active
   models**, by 4 (khaliq, ~135 Hz) and 3 (moubarak, ~90 Hz). Both are right
   about different things: COMPASS counts spike *peaks* inside the analysis
   window, eFEL counts spike *onsets*, and on a continuously firing cell the two
   conventions disagree at the window edges. Recorded with the measured
   difference, so a *change* in the gap fails the test even though the gap
   itself is understood.
5. **Four models fail gate N3 and are excluded from primary inference**
   (§5.0.1).
6. **Five sampled parameters across two models change nothing observable.**
   Gate N4 scales every exposed parameter by 1/4 and 4× and asks whether any
   extracted feature moves. `bbp_l5pc`'s `gskv3_apical` and `gcalva_apical` and
   `tomko_ca1`'s `gkdr_soma`, `gcal_soma` and `gcat_soma` do not. These are not
   plumbing bugs — the paired write test passes, so the value reaches the
   intended NEURON attribute with the intended magnitude — they are parameters
   the frozen protocol cannot observe. Both models remain in their cohorts and
   both are **failing N4**; §5.0.3 says what that costs.

First, the defect itself: the primary arm is now **N4-compliant by
projection**. Each N4-failing model's unidentifiable parameters are dropped
from the sampled space *before* ensembles, geometry and O-information are
computed, using the set gate N4 measured before any endpoint was inspected.

denominator alone (4/5 → 3/4), trading one defect for a smaller panel and a
threshold artefact. The cost of *not* projecting is measured by the
`n4_retained` arm rather than asserted away.

Second, the class of error: `tools/verify_report_numbers.py` now rejects the
sentence, not just the number. `pass(es) every gate`, `all gates pass`,
`gate-passing cohort`, `full N1–N7 enforcement`, `H-B is refuted` and `five
independent confirmations` are forbidden phrasings with a stated reason each,
because none of them can be true while any gate has a failing or missing
record. The cohort's own label is generated by
`compass.analysis.cohorts.cohort_label()` and asserted by
`tests/unit/test_audit_regressions.py`.

The replication cohort's net contribution is **two new source lineages**

(`tomko_ca1` inside `migliore_1999`). The audit asked for four to six new
lineages, and for three lineages in each class used for a class-specific claim.
Neither target is met: **no neuron class in the primary cohort has three
independent lineages**, so no class-specific claim is supportable, exactly as
before.

### 5.0.3 Models that fail gate N4 and are *not* excluded

Two implementations sample parameters the frozen protocol cannot observe:

| model | cohort | sampled | identifiable | unidentifiable |
|---|---|---:|---:|---|
| `bbp_l5pc` | discovery (**primary**) | 15 | 13 | `gskv3_apical`, `gcalva_apical` |
| `tomko_ca1` | replication | 16 | 13 | `gkdr_soma`, `gcal_soma`, `gcat_soma` |

The measurements are in
[`data/provenance/parameter_identifiability.json`](../data/provenance/parameter_identifiability.json).
Neither is an addressing bug: `test_n4_parameter_writes_reach_the_intended_target`
passes for both, so the value lands on the intended NEURON attribute with the
intended magnitude. For `tomko_ca1/gkdr_soma` the measured cause is a fourth
possibility the gate's failure message does not name — the effect on membrane
potential is real but below the resolution of the feature set. Scaling it by 1/4
and 4× moves `sum(v[::17])` from −72605.84 to −72567.27 and −72663.01, about
0.07%, while `spike_count` stays at 19 and `ap_halfwidth_ms` at 0.80 ms. Its
dendritic partner `gkdr_dend` — same mechanism, same attribute, 123 segments
instead of 1 — moves `spike_count` from 19 to 1. `bbp_l5pc`'s two are apical
conductances in a panel measured entirely at the soma, corroborated by a Sobol
total-order index of 0.00081 against 0.08–0.22 for active parameters.

**Why they are retained, and what is reported instead.** An unidentifiable
parameter is still drawn and still varies, so it contributes an unconstrained
dimension to the geometry that O-information and intrinsic dimension are computed
on. Removing it changes the sampled space and would require regenerating every
campaign that used it. Rather than assert that this does not matter, three
prespecified arms measure it:

| arm | what it does | why |
|---|---|---|
| `primary` | retains both models, **projects** their unidentifiable parameters out of the sampled space before ensembles are built | the headline rule (§0.0.1). Outcome-blind, and obtainable without re-simulating a trace |
| `replication_only` | the same projection, on the replication cohort | one N4 rule for both reported arms. This arm read the *unprojected* table until the 2026-08-09 post-response-3 audit (P0-5) found the registered rule applying to discovery and not to replication |
| `n4_strict` | excludes both models outright | the headline read under N1/N3/N4 enforcement (**not** "full N1–N7": N2 is evaluable for 6 of 14 models); drops the discovery cohort to 6 implementations over 4 lineages |
| `n4_retained` | retains both with their unidentifiable parameters intact | the former primary rule, kept so the cost of the projection is measured |
| `replication_n4_retained` | the same, on the replication cohort | the replication counterpart of `n4_retained` |

The disposition is computed **per arm and per model** by
`compass.analysis.cohorts.n4_disposition_by_arm()` and recorded in the release
manifest. It used to be inferred globally from whether the *primary* arm's
source-table name ended in `_n4proj`, which is how a projected discovery arm
came to certify an unprojected replication arm.

The projection is outcome-blind: the dropped set is read from the identifiability
record, which gate N4 measured before any endpoint was inspected. It requires its
own analysis pass, because — exactly like `drop_failed_feature` — a change to the
sampled space cannot be applied by filtering an endpoint table afterwards.

One prediction made during the previous round of this work was **wrong and is
withdrawn**: retained inert parameters were expected to inflate the affected
models' intrinsic dimension. They do not. `bbp_l5pc` sits at 0.889 intrinsic
dimensions per parameter, the second *lowest* of eight, so whatever the inert
dimensions do, they are not visible as an outlier at the endpoint level.

**This is the gate working, and it is expensive.** Two of the exclusions are
models that were fetched, adapted, had their mechanisms compiled, their f-I
curves calibrated and their convergence audited before being rejected. A gate
that never rejects anything that cost something is not doing any work. The
consequence is stated plainly rather than absorbed: the panel that actually
produces the headline numbers is smaller than the panel that was built, and the
two morphologically detailed replication candidates could not be run at a
convergence-accepted time step within this project's compute budget. That is a
real constraint on the audit's Priority-2 recommendation, not a detail.

### 5.1 Four models were re-simulated after a stricter convergence audit

The original per-model time steps were chosen by `tools/numerical_qc.py`, which
compares **spike count at the single canonical parameter point**. That criterion
is brittle: for `migliore_ca1` a 0.5 nA step sits on a knife edge where 4 vs 5
spikes is one boundary event in a 400 ms window.

`tools/convergence_audit.py` re-tested every model properly: **all 13
conditioning features, on 6 parameter points drawn from each model's own prior**,
against a reference at half the step on twice the mesh. A setting is accepted
only if every feature matches on every point.

| model | originally ran at | audit verdict | now runs at |
|---|---|---|---|
| `hh_cable`, `pospischil_rs`, `pospischil_fs` | dt 0.05 / 0.025 | accept | unchanged |
| `migliore_ca1` | dt 0.025, 1106 seg | **accept (1.000)** | unchanged |
| `hh_point` | dt 0.05 | reject (membrane tau) | dt 0.025 |
| `khaliq_purkinje` | dt 0.0125 | reject (tau, AP amplitude) | dt 0.00625 |
| `bbp_l5pc` | dt 0.05 | reject (AP amplitude) | dt 0.025 |
| `mainen_l5pc` | dt 0.025, 754 seg | reject (rate, AP amplitude) | dt 0.0125, **2304 seg** |

**Four of eight models had been running at unconverged settings** and the
canonical-point QC never saw it. They were re-simulated; the superseded shards
are retained under `results/features/screening_superseded_dt/` with a README
stating what each ran at and what it required. `bbp_l5pc` was re-run at half its
original sample size to fit the compute budget, which is why its N is 2,048.

### 5.1.1 The Mainen audit was itself inadequate, and Mainen is not converged

The audit above accepted `mainen_l5pc` at dt = 0.0125 ms on 2,304 segments. Its
reference was dt = 0.00625 ms **at that same 2,304-segment mesh**. A same-mesh
reference cannot detect spatial discretisation error, so what looked like a test
of the refined mesh was only ever a test of the time step at that mesh.

Re-run against a reference refined in **both** dimensions — dt = 0.00625 ms at
4× d_lambda, 4,440 segments, 75 min on 7 cores:

| setting | segments | mean agreement | features failing |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| dt 0.025, d_lambda mesh | 1,066 | 0.824 | 6 of 13 |
| dt 0.0125, d_lambda mesh | 1,066 | 0.824 | 6 of 13 |
| dt 0.025, production mesh | 2,304 | 0.989 | `ap_amplitude_mV` |
| **dt 0.0125 — production** | **2,304** | **0.989** | **`adaptation_index`** |

Two findings, pointing opposite ways, and both belong in the record.

**The refinement was strongly justified.** Doubling the d_lambda mesh moves
agreement from 0.824 to 0.989 and clears five of six failing features. The
adapter is right to refine, and the coarse mesh would have been a serious error.

**Mainen is nonetheless not converged.** No setting is accepted. At production,
one conditioning feature fails on one of seven prior samples, and the cause is a
single threshold-adjacent spike: `adaptation_index` is a ratio of ISIs, so
losing one spike from a 9-spike train swings it from −0.045 to +0.068.

`adaptation_index` is one of the 13 conditioning features, so **Mainen's
accepted ensemble is defined partly by a quantity that is not mesh-converged for
it**. This is reported rather than repaired: refining to 4,440 segments would
roughly double the cost of the most expensive model in the panel, and the same
threshold-adjacent behaviour would reappear at any finite mesh. It is also the
second independent reason to distrust this particular feature — it was already
withdrawn from the Sobol results for mean-imputation bias (§11.2).

**A separate defect in the criterion itself.** The tolerance for
`mean_firing_rate_Hz` was 2.0 Hz, but that readout is a spike count over a
400 ms window, whose quantum is 2.5 Hz. The criterion demanded a precision the
measurement cannot express, and no mesh could satisfy it: one threshold-adjacent
spike scored as a convergence failure at every rate below 40 Hz, while the same
physical event expressed as `spike_count` (tolerance one spike) scored as
agreement. Raised to exactly one quantum, from the protocol geometry, before
looking at any model's result — and it **does not change the verdict**, which is
how we know the correction was not a route to a wanted answer. The same quantum arithmetic once
supported a "measurement floor" reading of the perturbation results; that
reading is withdrawn (§11.1). It remains valid *here*, where the comparison is
between two single unaveraged observations of the same feature at two time
steps, which is exactly the case a per-observation quantum describes.

### 5.2 An explicit QC validity policy

20.2% of rows carry a QC flag. These are not all equivalent, so the policy is
declared rather than implicit (`src/compass/conditioning/ensembles.py`):

* **numerical failure** — `nonphysical_voltage`, `nonfinite_voltage`,
  `solver_failure`. Excluded from every ensemble under every policy. 891 rows,
  all in `khaliq_purkinje`.
* **phenotype observation** — `unstable_baseline`, `depolarised_rest`. The cell
  really did behave that way (it became spontaneously active or
  depolarisation-blocked); phenotype conditioning is the mechanism that should
  judge it. Retained under the `primary` policy, excluded under `strict`.

All results below use `primary`. The `strict` policy is available as a
sensitivity analysis and removes 27-67% of rows in the flag-heavy models.

### 5.3 The conditioning targets were built by a different integrator

The canonical manifests define the phenotype target that every ensemble is
accepted against. `tools/canonical_audit.py` took its integrator settings from
the first-pass QC sweep — **CVODE at dt = 0.025 ms for six of eight models** —
while the campaign runs **fixed-step at the per-model steps the feature-level
audit accepted** (§5.1). The repository held two solver contracts, and the
targets came from the one the campaign does not use.

Two things kept this alive. Nothing failed: both files were internally
consistent and written by working code. And the manifest builder could not be
re-run — it built every model in one process, which the adapters' cross-model
section guard blocks, so it died after the first model and had been unable to
finish since that guard landed (D29). The artefact was unreachable, not merely
unrevisited.

`CAMPAIGN_SOLVER` is now the single source of truth, the builder audits one
model per process, and `tests/unit/test_solver_contract.py` checks the **stored
manifests** — not the code that writes them — against the contract and against
the mesh the adapters actually build.

**The correction was not cosmetic.** Rebuilding under the campaign contract
moves the targets, and the movement is systematic:

| model | canonical spike count, CVODE → fixed-step | shift |
|---|---:|---:|
| `pospischil_fs` | 81 → 64 | −21%, 0.53 E1-SD |
| `hh_point` | 39 → 31 | −21%, 0.45 |
| `hh_cable` | 41 → 33 | −20%, 0.44 |
| `khaliq_purkinje` | 67 → 54 | −19%, 0.44 |
| `pospischil_rs` | 41 → 34 | −17%, 0.30 |
| `mainen_l5pc` | 17 → 13 | −24%, 0.25 |

**Every model's canonical spike count fell**, by 17–24%. That is a one-directional
bias, not scatter: the old target described a cell firing about a fifth faster
than the campaign's own integrator produces. "Accepted" had meant "close to a
phenotype this model does not exhibit under the settings its ensemble was
simulated with".

Most features barely moved — the median shift is 0.000–0.018 E1-SD and only
**3 of 96** feature-model pairs moved by more than 0.5 E1-SD. The damage is
concentrated in spike count and the quantities derived from it. But the size
matters relative to the conditioning tolerances, which are 3.0 / 2.0 / 1.0 /
0.5 z: a 0.44–0.53 SD target error is ~15% of the permissive tolerance and
**comparable to the entire very-strict tolerance**. The tightest tolerances —
already the ones with the fewest surviving models (§6.1) — were the most
affected. All conditioning-dependent results below are computed against the
corrected targets.

(One entry looks alarming and is not: `migliore_ca1`'s `sag_ratio` shifts 0.97
E1-SD, but both values round to 0.0000. Its E1 spread for that feature is
vanishingly small, so a physically negligible difference divides into a large
z. It is reported for completeness, not treated as a real move.)

### 5.4 Half the Sobol campaigns had been computed on shards from a different model

Sobol runs its own Saltelli design rather than reusing the screening ensembles,
and it decided whether to reuse a stored block with `if not out.exists()` —
nothing else. When `hh_point`, `khaliq_purkinje` and `bbp_l5pc` were
re-simulated at corrected time steps (§5.1), their Sobol shards, produced under
the old settings, were silently kept. Nothing failed. The indices were simply
computed on a different model from the one the rest of this document describes.

Every shard now carries a sidecar identity: solver contract, design, feature
list, and a code hash scoped to the modules that can change a simulated value.
A shard whose identity does not match is moved to `_superseded/<timestamp>/` and
re-run — never deleted, never silently reused.

**Stored shards were not stamped retroactively.** Writing a stamp onto an
artefact whose provenance cannot be read from the artefact asserts exactly the
thing in question — the mistake that destroyed the Mainen convergence evidence
(§5.1.1). Instead `--verify-stored` re-simulates one real block per model under
the current code and compares it bit-for-bit:

| model | verdict | action |
|---|---|---|
| `hh_point`, `khaliq_purkinje`, `bbp_l5pc` | differ on **all 7** features | quarantined, 132 blocks re-run |
| `hh_cable`, `pospischil_rs`, `pospischil_fs` | reproduce **bit-for-bit** | stamped, not re-run |

The split matched the four models §5.1 had re-simulated, which is the outcome
the design predicted but not one that was assumed. The three verified models
then reproduced their previous interaction fractions to three decimals, while
`bbp_l5pc` moved from 0.482 to 0.538 — so the verification saved re-running 208
valid blocks without hiding a real change.

### 5.5 Retained waveforms: what feature replay could not see

Deterministic replay re-simulates a stored sample and compares the extracted
features. It is a strong check of determinism and it cannot detect an implausible
trace, because a trace that is wrong in the same way twice reproduces perfectly.
The 2026-08-09 audit made that objection and asked for archived voltage.

`tools/retain_waveforms.py` retains a stratified sample — every model × regime ×
campaign block, plus the risk strata `validate_replay` already defines — and runs
eight checks that features cannot express: voltage range, baseline stability,
spike shape, spike-count agreement, depolarisation-block detection, single-step
discontinuity, finiteness, and recomputation of every feature from the retained
trace.

**The 2026-08-09 post-response-2 audit found that the last of those was not what
it claimed** (§0.0.1). Features were recomputed from the in-memory,
full-precision trace, and only afterwards was a float32 copy written to disk —
so nothing demonstrated that the *archive* reproduces the stored row. The
procedure now writes the archive at float64 with a self-contained metadata
sidecar, **reloads it**, verifies its key and array inventory, and recomputes
every feature from the reloaded arrays. Spike amplitude and half-width are
measured by an independent detector running on the archived trace rather than
read out of the stored feature row, and the detector's spike count is compared
against the stored one inside the extractor's own analysis window. Every
simulation, archive-write, reload, schema or recomputation error is a **failing**
gate record; the previous version returned an empty problem list for a simulation
error, which `gate_record` reads as PASS.

A second defect surfaced while implementing that: the battery was globbing
`results/features/<campaign>/**/*.parquet`, which includes the `_quarantine`
directory that `validate_campaign` has always excluded. The replication campaign
alone contributed 18,944 duplicate (model, regime, replicate, sample) keys, so a
retained trace could be checked against a superseded row. Fixed; the numbers
below are from the regenerated battery.

**916 traces across five campaigns, and the recomputation result is the
important one: 44,887 features recomputed from the reloaded float64 archives,
zero mismatches.** The archived trace really does regenerate the stored row.

| campaign | selected | clean | flagged | features recomputed | mismatched |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `screening` | 444 | 395 | 49 | 22,076 | 0 |
| `screening_scramble2` | 205 | 168 | 37 | 9,413 | 0 |
| `sobol` | 151 | 127 | 24 | 7,329 | 0 |
| `screening_v2_replication` | 95 | 63 | 32 | 4,975 | 0 |
| `screening_expand` | 21 | 18 | 3 | 1,094 | 0 |
| **total** | **916** | **771** | **145** | **44,887** | **0** |

The 145 flagged samples carry **325 findings** between them — a sample can fail
more than one check on more than one protocol, which is why the two counts must
never be conflated: `spike_shape` 169, `baseline_stability` 128,
`voltage_range` 23, `depolarisation_block` 5. Every class has a disposition
fixed before the run, recorded in each record's `disposition` block.

Two of them are properties of the campaign design rather than coding errors,
and **neither is repaired** — the QC thresholds are frozen properties of the
campaign, and moving one after seeing which samples it admits is an
outcome-driven change that would also invalidate every stored shard.

**1. Sub-physiological hyperpolarisation (23 samples, 2.5%).** Sixteen are
`khaliq_purkinje` and seven `dougalis_vlpag_da`, on the hyperpolarising step,
reaching between −192 and −225 mV. This is Ohm's law rather than a numerical
failure: `khaliq_purkinje`'s input resistance has a median of 593 MΩ and a 99th
percentile of 2,361 MΩ against a median of 23–49 MΩ elsewhere, and the step is
a fixed −0.05 nA.

**Under one band these traces are flagged here and their rows are excluded from
the corpus — the same rule, both places.** That is the substantive change from
the previous revision, which ran two bands: the corpus excluded outside
[−160, +120] mV while this battery flagged outside [−150, +80], leaving a 10 mV
window in which a trace was "physiologically impossible" in this section and
fully analysis-eligible in the inference. §0.0.2 records the unification and
its cost — 609 rows leave the analysis set, 397 `khaliq_purkinje` and 212
`dougalis_vlpag_da`, and none enter. That the battery's own findings land on
**exactly those two models** is the independent corroboration: the band was not
tightened to chase a number, and the rows it removes are the ones whose traces
this detector, running on reloaded archives, calls impossible.

The affected quantities are the hyperpolarisation-derived features (input
resistance, sag, membrane tau); the `step_abs_hi` spike features that carry most
of the conditioning phenotype are untouched. A reviewer who prefers the wider
band can read the consequence rather than take this on trust: the
`wide_voltage_band` sensitivity arm re-admits the discovery campaign's share of
those rows and reports what changes.

**2. Slow baseline settling drift (69 samples, 7.5%).** The pre-stimulus mean is
still moving when the step arrives: the mean over the last 50 ms before onset
differs from the earlier pre-stimulus mean by more than 10% of the trace's total
excursion. This is a *different* defect from the one QC catches — the
`unstable_baseline` flag tests the peak-to-peak range of the second half of the
pre-stimulus window against 2.0 mV, which is a ripple detector, and a membrane
settling smoothly has small ripple while its mean is still moving. It is the
same failure mode already documented for `ortner_snc_da` under gate N6, where an
unsettled baseline plus an Ih sag produced a *negative* input resistance.
Distributed across eight models: `bbp_l5pc` 20, `pospischil_rs` 17,
`dougalis_vlpag_da` 14, `pospischil_fs` 11, `khaliq_purkinje` 3,
`mainen_l5pc` 2, `tomko_ca1` 1, `moubarak_snc_da` 1.

Both point at the same design decision: a **fixed absolute stimulus amplitude
and a fixed pre-stimulus delay**, applied unchanged across a prior wide enough
to move input resistance by two orders of magnitude. The next campaign should
set the hyperpolarising amplitude and the settling period per model from
measured passive properties instead.

## 6. Result 1 — conditioning manufactures dependence

Every ensemble below starts from the **same** independent quasi-random prior, in
which parameters are uncorrelated by construction. Across all 8 models, the
unconditioned E1 ensemble has O-information indistinguishable from zero at every
tuple order — the estimator's null is clean.

Tightening the phenotype tolerance moves every model toward synergy (mean over
the models available at each tolerance; Gaussian estimator, absolute protocols,
max-z distance):

| tolerance | models | resolvable | acceptance | Δ mean \|r\| vs E1 | Δ net O-information |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| permissive (3.0 z) | 8 | 5 | 53% | +0.051 | −0.00019 |
| moderate (2.0 z) | 8 | 6 | 41% | +0.068 | −0.00052 |
| strict (1.0 z) | 6 | 2 | 10% | +0.129 | −0.00218 |
| very strict (0.5 z) | 3 | 1 | 2% | +0.291 | **+0.00199** |

The last row is positive, and §6.1 explains why it is not a finding.

**Direction agreement at the permissive tolerance is complete: all 8 of 8 models
move toward synergy**, from a 1-compartment Hodgkin-Huxley cell to a
2,304-compartment reconstruction. That is the claim the decision criterion
scores, and it stands.

### 6.0 Is the effect above the estimator's own noise?

Eight arrows pointing the same way is only evidence if each arrow is longer than
its own error bar, and these effects are numerically tiny — Δ ranges from
−0.0008 to −0.000002. Two checks, both necessary.

**The estimator is unbiased under this procedure.** The unconditioned E1
ensembles give net O-information **−0.0000000 ± 0.000009** and the
column-shuffled E8 null gives **+0.000008 ± 0.000025** (means ± SD over 8
models). Conditioning does not push this statistic negative by construction, so
a negative shift is not an artefact of the machinery.

**Each model's shift is compared with its own bootstrap noise**, resampling that
model's accepted ensemble at its own size (`tools/estimator_reliability.py`):

| model | n (E3) | Δ net O-info | bootstrap SD | \|Δ\|/SD |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `hh_cable` | 2,743 | −0.000250 | 0.000049 | **5.1σ** |
| `pospischil_fs` | 6,642 | −0.000069 | 0.000014 | **5.0σ** |
| `hh_point` | 2,633 | −0.000804 | 0.000164 | **4.9σ** |
| `pospischil_rs` | 5,247 | −0.000071 | 0.000014 | **4.9σ** |
| `khaliq_purkinje` | 2,326 | −0.000064 | 0.000016 | **4.1σ** |
| `migliore_ca1` | 140 | −0.000254 | 0.000262 | 1.0σ |
| `mainen_l5pc` | 153 | −0.000031 | 0.000092 | 0.3σ |
| `bbp_l5pc` | 321 | −0.000002 | 0.000018 | 0.1σ |

**Five of eight implementations exceed their own conditional bootstrap noise by a factor of 4-5.** These are conditional bootstrap signal-to-noise ratios, **not sigma significances**: they bootstrap only the already-selected E3 parameters, hold the E1 endpoint fixed, do not repeat conditioning or target estimation, do not propagate tuple or surrogate choices, and rest on 40 replicates for the per-model SD (2026-08-06 audit, 7.3). The three that do not are
exactly the three smallest ensembles (140, 153, 321 samples): they are
sample-size limited, not effect limited, and their direction still agrees.

The σ ratios cluster at 4–5 across models whose absolute effects span a 13-fold
range (−0.0008 to −0.00006). Effect and noise therefore scale together here, so
an effect being numerically small is not by itself an objection to it — but nor
is the clustering evidence of a shared mechanism, since both quantities depend
on the same ensemble size and spread.

So Result 1 should be read as: **the direction is consistent across all eight
models and individually significant in the five with adequate samples.** The
magnitude is a property of the ensemble construction, not a quantity to compare
between models.

### 6.1 The dose-response holds where it is measurable, and only there

The original release headed this section "monotonically" and read the table above
as a clean dose-response. A first correction replaced that with "only 4 of 8
models are monotone, and three reverse sign". With per-model bootstrap noise now
available (§6.0), **both statements are wrong in the same way**: they treated
every number as a measurement, when most of the tight-tolerance numbers are not
resolvable at all.

Per-model Δ net O-information, each with its significance against that model's
own bootstrap noise:

| model | permissive | moderate | strict | very strict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `hh_cable` | −0.00025 **5.1σ** | −0.00104 **8.1σ** | −0.00217 **8.9σ** | −0.00440 **5.3σ** |
| `hh_point` | −0.00080 **4.9σ** | −0.00158 **3.8σ** | −0.01120 **7.1σ** | −0.00483 *0.7σ* |
| `pospischil_rs` | −0.00007 **4.9σ** | −0.00025 **6.1σ** | −0.00033 *0.7σ* | — |
| `pospischil_fs` | −0.00007 **5.0σ** | −0.00009 **3.0σ** | +0.00065 *1.5σ* | +0.01521 *2.0σ* |
| `khaliq_purkinje` | −0.00006 **4.1σ** | −0.00006 **3.0σ** | −0.00006 *0.3σ* | — |
| `migliore_ca1` | −0.00025 *1.0σ* | −0.00114 *2.2σ* | — | — |
| `mainen_l5pc` | −0.00003 *0.3σ* | +0.00001 *0.0σ* | — | — |
| `bbp_l5pc` | −0.00000 *0.1σ* | −0.00003 *0.5σ* | +0.00005 *0.2σ* | — |

Three things follow, and they are more specific than either earlier claim.

**Where the effect is resolvable, it is monotone.** `hh_cable` is significant at
all four tolerances (5.1 → 8.1 → 8.9 → 5.3σ) and its magnitude rises
monotonically, −0.00025 → −0.00440, a 17-fold increase. `hh_point` is monotone
across its three resolvable tolerances. No model that can measure the effect
contradicts the dose-response.

**Every apparent reversal is noise.** All four positive entries sit at ≤2.0σ:
`mainen_l5pc` +0.00001 (0.0σ), `bbp_l5pc` +0.00005 (0.2σ), `pospischil_fs`
+0.00065 (1.5σ) and +0.01521 (2.0σ). The earlier claim that "three models,
including both cortical reconstructions, reverse sign" described sampling
scatter around zero. Matched subsampling confirms it directly: at n = 66 this
statistic flips sign in up to 33% of resamples of ensembles whose full-sample
value is clearly negative, reaching +0.0066 by chance
(`estimator_reliability.json`).

**The panel mean at very-strict is not a quantity.** It is +0.00199, positive,
and it is positive only because the corrected targets (§5.3) admitted
`pospischil_fs`'s 66-sample ensemble at +0.01521 — a single 2.0σ value in an
ensemble of 66 points in 7 dimensions, outweighing two well-resolved negative
ones. Resolvable coverage across the four tolerances is **5 → 6 → 2 → 1**: by
the tightest tolerance only `hh_cable` can measure anything.

So the honest reading is narrower than the original and less nihilistic than the
first correction: **conditioning drives the ensemble toward synergy, and
tightening it increases the effect monotonically in every model with the samples
to see it. The panel-level dose-response curve remains not estimable**, because
the panel composition changes with tolerance and resolution collapses as
acceptance falls.

Fixing this needs a balanced design — per-model tolerances chosen to equalise
acceptance, or the trend estimated only on models present and resolvable at every
level — plus a **minimum-sample policy for entropy endpoints**, which the project
currently lacks. Sobol has `VALID_FRACTION_FLOOR`; the conditioning path has no
equivalent, so a 66-sample ensemble votes equally with a 6,642-sample one. No
such threshold is applied retroactively here: choosing one after seeing which
values it removes is the failure mode recorded in D19.

## 7. Result 2 — the naive synergy statistic is not a statistic

The quantity usually reported — the fraction of tuples with Ω < 0 — is compared
here against a family-wise surrogate distribution built from column-shuffled
ensembles evaluated over the *same number of tuples*.

| ensemble | naive fraction Ω<0 | surrogate-controlled |
|---|---:|---:|
| **E8 (shuffled null: dependence destroyed by construction)** | **0.455** | **0.000** |
| E3 (phenotype-conditioned) | 0.621 | 0.270 |

An ensemble in which all dependence has been *deliberately destroyed* still
reports **45% "synergy-dominated" tuples**. Surrogate control correctly returns
**0.000** — at every order, for every model.

**Any report of "N% of tuples were synergy-dominated" without a matched-search
surrogate control is uninterpretable.**

## 8. Result 3 — the regime, not the neuron, sets the sign

Direction agreement across models, per contrast:

| contrast | models agreeing |
|---|---|
| conditioning → synergy (permissive tolerance) | **8 / 8** |
| conditioning → synergy (averaged over all four tolerances) | 7 / 8 |
| high-covariance subset (E7) → redundancy | **8 / 8** |
| covariance-preserving prior (E2) → redundancy | **6 / 6** |
| clustered prior (E6) → synergy | **6 / 6** |

The two conditioning rows differ for the reason given in §6.1: averaging over
tolerances gives 66-sample ensembles the same weight as 6,642-sample ones, and
one model's mean turns positive on a 2.0σ fluctuation. The permissive row is the
one where every model has the samples to resolve its own sign.

Net O-information varies **1.8×** more within one model across regimes
(SD 0.0052) than within one regime across models (SD 0.0029).

**E7's marginal balance, per cell.** The high-covariance subset is constructed
under a constraint — no parameter's subset mean may sit more than 0.10 parent SD
from the parent mean — and the search **relaxes that constraint when no candidate
satisfies it**, returning the least-imbalanced selection instead. Until the
2026-08-09 audit the endpoint table carried no trace of which of those two had
happened for any given cell, so a reader could not tell a matched E7 from a
relaxed one. Every endpoint row now carries the achieved balance and a boolean:

| | E7 cells | max mean shift |
|---|---:|---|
| constraint satisfied | **13 / 15** | ≤ 0.0998 SD |
| constraint relaxed | 2 / 15 | `migliore_ca1` absolute **0.1310 SD**, `mainen_l5pc` absolute **0.1208 SD** |

The audit identified the Migliore cell from the outside and was right about the
value. Both relaxed cells are *absolute*-family; both models' normalised cells
satisfy the constraint. Filter on `e7_balance_satisfied` to exclude them.

The separate balance audit in `tools/run_robustness.py` was **auditing a
different E7 than the one that was published**: it rebuilt the ensembles with
`seed=1` while `tools/run_analysis.py` builds them with `20260804`, so it could
not have detected an imbalance in the stored selection. It now uses the analysis
seed and the primary QC policy, and reports, over 72 parameters:
mean KS distance 0.064, max 0.183, 15.3% of parameters KS-significant at
p < 0.05, SD ratio in [0.79, 1.28].

**Two different numbers are in play and they are not comparable.** The
constraint (0.10 SD, satisfied by 13/15 cells above) measures E7 against its
**parent accepted set**. The audit's `max_abs_mean_shift_sd` of **0.264**
measures E7 against **E3m**, the size-matched *random* subset that is its
comparator — two subsets of the same parent, so their difference can be roughly
twice either one's shift from the parent. Quoting one as if it violated the
other's threshold would be wrong in both directions.

### 8.1 Mean correlation does not determine the balance

| regime | mean \|r\| | net O-information | order-5 tuples significantly… |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| E2 covariance-preserving prior | 0.270 | **+0.0109** | mostly **redundant** |
| E6 clustered prior | 0.291 | **−0.0170** | mostly **synergistic** |

Two ensembles at essentially the same mean pairwise correlation land on
**opposite sides**. What differs is the *shape* of the dependence: E2 is a
smooth banded copula over the full prior, E6 a union of narrow isolated basins.
Covariance magnitude is not the mechanism; ensemble geometry is.

## 9. Result 4 — variance partitioning, with cluster-aware uncertainty

Commonality analysis over predictor blocks (unique, semi-partial R²) gives a
point ratio of **18.6** for sampling+geometry versus model identity on
`synergy_area` (unique R² 0.176 + 0.013 versus 0.010). That single number
understates the uncertainty: the ~200 endpoint rows are not independent
experiments, because E3/E4/E7/E8/E9 are all *derived* from the same E1 campaign.
The inferential unit is the **source lineage**, so the ratio is re-estimated with
a bootstrap over lineages:

| table | clusters | ratio median | 95% CI | resamples exceeding the preregistered 2.0 |
|---|---:|---:|---|---:|
| raw endpoints | 6 lineages | 13.1 | [3.2, 68.9] | **100%** |
| **size-matched** (20 draws, common N per regime) | 5 lineages | **14.4** | **[2.8, 89.7]** | **100%** |

> Both rows moved again under the probe-4 regeneration (§0.0.2); they read 13.4
> [3.3, 70.1] and 16.5 [3.4, 105.9] on the probe-3 rows, and 11.5
> [2.7, 46.9] and 12.8 [2.6, 46.1] before that. Before that they had
> moved when the bootstrap unit was corrected from `model_id` to
> `source_lineage_id` (2026-08-09 audit), where they read 11.9 [3.3, 71.2]
> and 12.6 [3.6, 60.5] over 8 and 7 *implementations*. The point estimates barely
> move; the intervals do, and the lower bounds fall — which is the expected
> direction, because the HH and Pospischil lineages were each supplying two draws
> that are not independent. The preregistered threshold of 2.0 is still exceeded
> in every resample.

The size-matched table is the one that answers the review's F4: every model is
subsampled to a common N per regime and the endpoints recomputed 20 times, so
"model identity" is no longer confounded with "how many samples that model got".
The matched design additionally pools `screening_expand` into `migliore_ca1` and
withholds the four implementations that fail gate N3, so a model excluded from
every other primary analysis can no longer bind a matched cell through the
`min()` that sets the common N.

Under that design the ratio is **14.4 with a proper interval**, above the raw
figure of 13.1 rather than below it. The earlier matched estimate of 9.3
was depressed by a gate-failed model sitting inside the design and by the small
common N that model forced; removing it and enlarging the binding model's sample
raises the matched ratio rather than lowering it. It remains well below the 22.6
originally reported as an unmatched point estimate, the preregistered threshold
of 2.0 is exceeded in **every single resample**, and the interval is wide
because seven models is a small bootstrap unit, not because the effect is
fragile.

A mixed model with model family as a random effect decomposes `synergy_area`
into a fixed part (regime, geometry, sample size), a between-family random part,
and residual:

| quantity | value | meaning |
|---|---:|---|
| variance-partition coefficient | 0.055 | between-family share of **total** variance |
| **adjusted ICC** | **0.262** | between-family share of the variance **remaining after** the fixed covariates |
| marginal R² | 0.790 | share explained by the fixed covariates alone |
| conditional R² | 0.845 | share explained by fixed + family effects |

An earlier version of this document reported a single "intraclass correlation of
**0.048**" and glossed it as "under 5% of endpoint variance lies between model
families". Two corrections (audit M7).

**The number was computed wrongly.** The fixed-effect variance was taken as
`np.var(m.fittedvalues)`, but statsmodels' `fittedvalues` for a mixed model
already contain the random-effect predictions, so the family variance was
counted once inside that term and again as its own term. The denominator was
inflated and the result was neither of the two standard quantities.

**The right ICC is several times larger.** Computed correctly, the adjusted ICC
is **0.262** — roughly 26% of the *residual* variance is between families, not
under 5%. (It read 0.138 on the pre-probe-3 rows; the probe-3 regeneration
raised it, which is the expected direction — 1,867 rows that had been recorded
as silent are now spontaneously firing or have a measured rheobase, and they are
concentrated in three lineages.) The 0.023 figure is the variance-partition coefficient, a different
and much weaker statement: family identity is a small share of total variance
mainly because the fixed covariates already explain 83% of it. (On the previous,
CVODE-built conditioning targets these were 0.289 and 0.050; correcting the
targets in §5.3 reduced the between-family share, which is consistent with part
of the apparent family structure having been an artefact of a target computed by
the wrong integrator.)

**It has therefore been withdrawn as a decision criterion**, and not only
because the arithmetic was wrong. The fixed covariates conditioned on here —
sampling regime, geometry, sample size — *are* the H-G hypothesis. Regressing
them out first and then asking whether the residual favours H-B tests H-B
against a model that has already been given the alternative. The quantity is
still written to `mixed_effects.json` and reported above; it no longer votes.

**Leave-one-model-family-out** prediction of `synergy_area`, all eight folds
reported rather than the median alone:

| held-out model | R² | | held-out model | R² |
|---|---:|---|---|---:|
| `hh_cable` | +0.982 | | `pospischil_rs` | +0.804 |
| `khaliq_purkinje` | +0.842 | | `hh_point` | +0.616 |
| `mainen_l5pc` | +0.835 | | `bbp_l5pc` | +0.463 |
| `pospischil_fs` | +0.833 | | **`migliore_ca1`** | **−0.748** |

Median **0.818**, mean **0.578**: transfer works for seven families and still
fails for `migliore_ca1`, the smallest ensemble in the panel (256 samples).
Reporting only the median would hide that.

The failure is **much less severe than the previous release reported** (−4.468,
mean 0.057). Correcting the conditioning targets (§5.3) improved out-of-sample
transfer for every model, which is the behaviour expected if the old targets
were adding model-specific error: a target computed by the wrong integrator is a
per-model offset, and per-model offsets are exactly what a held-out fold cannot
learn.

**Classifier test.** Sampling regime is recoverable across *unseen model
families* at 0.447 vs a 0.155 baseline (11 classes). Model identity is
recoverable at 0.585 vs 0.146 (8 classes) — information summaries do carry
model-specific signal.

## 10. Result 5 — target-specific information behaves differently

O-information describes dependence *among parameters*. RSI asks whether a tuple
carries more information about an electrical feature jointly than separately.
Fraction of order-3 tuples with RSI > 0 is already **0.76** in the *unconditioned
independent* prior, where parameters are uncorrelated by construction and no
conditioning artefact is available — and conditioning slightly *reduces* it
(E3 0.711, E3b 0.698, E4 0.692) even as it drives O-information the other way.

This is the separation the blueprint insists on: dependence among sampled
parameters and target-specific information are different objects, and here they
respond to conditioning in opposite directions.

## 11. Result 6 — statistical synergy points at functionally interacting parameters, but the inference is unresolved

> **This heading used to read "statistical synergy DOES identify functionally
> interacting parameters".** It was written when the paired tests appeared to
> separate from unity over seven implementations. At the lineage level they do
> not, and the criteria are now withheld from the decision pending a numerical
> repeatability check (§11.1.1, §12.0). The measurement in this section stands;
> the inference it was being used to support does not.

This section reverses the conclusion of the first release. The original
perturbation experiment had three design faults, all identified by review:
backgrounds were drawn from E1 filtered only for "fires at all" rather than from
the accepted manifold (only 49 of 64 were E3 members); 279 shifted parameter
values left the declared prior; and η was normalised by the SD of the
unconditioned ensemble rather than of the population being probed.

The corrected battery draws backgrounds by k-means from the **E3 accepted
ensemble itself**, keeps every displacement inside the prior by flipping its sign
inward when necessary (**0 out-of-prior evaluations**, 1,132 flips), normalises
on the tested population, evaluates **four features** from the same simulations,
and adds a **uniformly random control family** alongside the matched
middle-Ω one. All numbers below are computed against the corrected conditioning
targets (§5.3), which moved the accepted ensembles the backgrounds are drawn
from.

Primary feature (mean firing rate), 7 models × 18 tuples × 8 backgrounds:

| | top-synergy | middle-Ω control | random control |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| mean \|η\| (ensemble SD) | **0.189** | 0.115 | 0.068 |
| median \|η\| | 0.090 | 0.042 | 0.029 |
| meeting the functional-support bar | **20.8%** | 8.3% | 2.1% |

| comparison | ratio | 95% CI (lineage bootstrap) | Wilcoxon p | lineages with top > control |
|---|---:|---|---:|---:|
| top vs **random** | 2.34× | [1.18, 5.75] | 0.156 | 5 / 6 |
| top vs **middle-Ω** | 1.43× | [0.84, 2.99] | 0.562 | 3 / 6 |

> **These are the probe-4 numbers and they are weaker than the probe-3 ones
> they replace (3.83× and 2.49×, p = 0.063 and 0.188, over five lineages).** Two
> things moved, and they are separable. First, `mainen_1996` **entered the
> battery**: under probe-3 its accepted ensemble was too small to draw
> backgrounds from, under probe-4 it is not, and it is the one lineage whose
> top-synergy tuples are *less* non-additive than their controls (0.047 vs
> 0.113 SD). Dropping it recovers 1.62×. Second, of the five lineages present
> under both corpora, three are numerically **identical** (`hay_2011`,
> `migliore_1999`, `pospischil_2008`) and two moved: the HH lineage's
> top-tuple non-additivity halved (0.747 → 0.377) and `khaliq_2003`'s *control*
> rose ninefold (0.012 → 0.116). Those two are exactly where the corpus changed
> most (§0.0.2).
>
> An earlier revision replaced model-level figures of 4.74× (p = 0.016) and
> 2.97× (p = 0.047) with lineage-clustered ones, because the bootstrap unit was
> `model_id` and the HH and Pospischil lineages each supplied two of seven
> supposedly independent units. The claim that both paired tests had fallen
> below 0.05 was withdrawn then and stays withdrawn.

**The direction is no longer safe either.** The battery now runs on all eight
implementations with a usable accepted ensemble — six lineages, `mainen_l5pc`
included, so this arm no longer carries the partial-coverage caveat. Five of six
lineages still show top > random, but only **three of six** show top > middle-Ω,
and the matched interval **[0.84, 2.99] includes 1.0**. Against the random
control the interval excludes 1.0 and the direction survives; against the
matched control it does not.

When the H-B criterion is scored it is judged on the lineage-clustered paired
difference over the *repeatable* subset, not on these point ratios — and there
(§12.0) it is **−0.002 SD, 95% CI [−0.023, +0.037] over three lineages**: no
enrichment at all.

The continuous relationship weakens but keeps its sign: across the 144
primary-readout tuples, more synergistic tuples are more non-additive,
**r = −0.386** (it was −0.515 on 126 probe-3 tuples).

**What can be said.** Against a *uniformly random* control, statistically
synergistic tuples are enriched for functional non-additivity by about 2.3×,
with a lineage-bootstrap interval that excludes no-enrichment. Against the
*matched middle-Ω* control — the comparison that controls for how much a tuple's
parameters move anything at all — the point estimate is 1.43× with an interval
spanning no-enrichment, only half the lineages agree in direction, and on the
numerically repeatable subset the effect is zero. **The matched contrast is the
one the preregistered criterion uses, and it fails.** The enrichment is also
absent for waveform readouts. The "unresolvable in half the panel" companion
claim remains withdrawn as invalid.

For the record, the *original* pre-audit numbers (1.40×, 12.5% supported vs
14.6% for controls, r = −0.064) were an artefact of a flawed design and remain
withdrawn; the correction of that design was real. It is the strength of the
replacement claim that has since been revised downward **three times**: once by
the corrected battery, once by counting lineages instead of implementations, and
once by requiring the underlying η to be numerically repeatable.

**But that correlation exists only for spike-timing readouts.** Computed
separately on each of the four features measured from the *same* simulations:

| readout | kind of quantity | r(Ω, \|η\|) |
|---|---|---:|
| mean firing rate | spike **count** in a window | **−0.515** |
| first ISI | spike **time** difference | **−0.318** |
| AP amplitude | continuous voltage shape | −0.163 |
| AP half-width | continuous voltage shape | **−0.006** |

The link between statistical synergy and functional non-additivity is present
for the two readouts derived from spike timing and **essentially absent** for
the two derived from waveform shape — AP half-width is −0.006, indistinguishable
from no relationship. The same split appears in the group means. Pooled
enrichment over the matched middle-Ω control, by readout: **2.97×** for firing
rate and **2.79×** for first ISI, against **1.32×** for AP amplitude and
**0.85×** for AP half-width — the waveform readouts show little or no
separation, and half-width runs slightly the wrong way.

The split has survived every correction this project has made to the battery —
the target correction, the regeneration, the change of bootstrap unit — while
the enrichment ratios have moved substantially each time. That makes it the most
robust finding in this section.

This is the central methodological finding of the project, and §14 develops it:
the tuples are the same, the simulations are the same, and the parameter
perturbations are identical. Only the measurement taken from the resulting
voltage trace differs, and it decides whether "these parameters form a
functional module" is true or false.

### 11.1 WITHDRAWN: the "measurement floor" analysis, and what replaces it

**This section previously reported that the enrichment was carried by two toy
models and was unresolvable in four models including both cortical
reconstructions. That analysis was invalid and every conclusion from it is
withdrawn** (2026-08-06 audit §4).

What it did. The primary readout is a spike count over a 400 ms step, so one
extra spike is 2.5 Hz. The old analysis divided that quantum by each model's
rate SD to get a "floor", and compared it against the per-model **median over
six tuple means of eight backgrounds**.

Why that tests nothing. Three separate errors, any one of which is fatal:

1. **The units are aligned but the estimators are not.** The reported statistic
   is a mean over eight backgrounds. Its native grid is 2.5/8 = **0.3125 Hz**,
   and the per-model median over six such tuple means can fall on a 0.15625 Hz
   grid. Comparing a median of averages against a single unaveraged quantum
   compares two different sampling operations.
2. **A quantisation grid is not a detection limit.** The quantum is
   deterministic. A mean of 0.05 SD is exactly observable and exactly
   repeatable; nothing about a discrete sample space makes a small average
   unmeasurable. A stochastic floor would need measurement error, simulation
   variability or background variability — the boolean `resolvable` column
   tested none of them.
3. **It answered a question nobody asked.** "Can this readout represent this
   value?" is not "is this effect distinguishable from zero?"

Retired with it: *"four of eight models are unresolvable"*, *"the panel-level
enrichment is carried by the two Hodgkin–Huxley toy models"*, and *"both
multicompartment cortical reconstructions fall below a hard floor"*. The tool
and its outputs are preserved, clearly labelled, under
`results/analyses/_withdrawn/`.

**What replaces it.** The two questions the old analysis conflated are now asked
separately, each with a definition fixed in advance
(`tools/perturbation_inference.py` → `perturbation_inference.json`).

*Numerical repeatability* — would this number survive a finer integration? The
whole battery is re-run at **half the campaign time step** and per-background η
is compared directly. Repeatable means median |Δη| ≤ 0.05 feature SD *and* ≥ 80%
of tuple means keeping their sign. This is a property of the solver, and it is
measured rather than asserted.

*Inferential precision* — is the background mean distinguishable from zero, and
how large is it? Estimated from the spread **across backgrounds**: a percentile
bootstrap of the background mean, an exact sign test on background-level η
(distribution-free, so quantisation costs it nothing), and — for the count
readout — an **exact enumeration of all 2⁸ = 256 background sign flips**, so the
p-value carries no Monte-Carlo error.

*Counts are analysed as counts.* `mean_firing_rate_Hz` is a spike count divided
by a fixed window. The battery now stores `spike_count` as well, and
`η_count = (N_joint − N_base) − Σᵢ (Nᵢ − N_base)` is analysed on the integer
scale, where discreteness is a property of the sample space rather than a
nuisance to be compared against.

*Raw outcomes are archived.* `perturbations_raw.parquet` holds one row per
model × tuple × background × condition × feature, with the baseline, each
single, the joint, the **signed displacement actually applied** (the battery
flips a step inward at the prior boundary, so the design is not recoverable from
the nominal δ) and the simulation/QC status. The previous run computed those
arrays and discarded them, which is precisely why the invalid analysis could not
be re-examined without re-simulating.

**What survives.** The defensible perturbation summary is unchanged by the
withdrawal and is no more positive for it: top tuples have a larger point mean
absolute non-additivity than either control family, the paired lineage-clustered
tests are inconclusive, and 31.0% of top tuples meet the preregistered
functional-support rule against 7.1% of controls. Removing an invalid negative
claim does not create a positive one.

### 11.2 Sobol: interactions are pervasive, but the headline feature was an artefact

The Saltelli estimator mean-imputes outputs that are undefined for a given
sample. That missingness is **not** missing-at-random — a feature is undefined
precisely when the sample fails to produce the phenotype — and it biases
`1 − ΣS₁` upward. Only analyses with ≥90% finite outputs are quoted:

| feature | valid fraction | interaction fraction | quotable analyses |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| mean firing rate | 1.00 | 0.270 | 3 |
| rheobase | 0.97 | 0.153 | 1 |
| input resistance | 1.00 | 0.069 | 3 |
| resting potential | 1.00 | 0.012 | 1 |

Three of the seven features — AP amplitude, AP half-width and spike-frequency
adaptation — have **no quotable analysis on any model**, so they are absent from
the table rather than shown with a struck-through value.

**Quotability now requires precision, not only completeness** (2026-08-06 audit,
M8). The previous rule was `valid_fraction >= 0.90` alone, under which an index
counted as quotable while its bootstrap confidence half-width exceeded the
estimate itself, two "quotable" interaction fractions were negative, and the
mean `ST - S1` exceeded 1 — all signatures of estimator noise rather than of
interaction mass. An analysis is now quotable only if it is **complete**
(valid fraction ≥ 0.90), **precise** (the widest S1 confidence half-width is at
most half the largest S1 it describes) and **physically plausible**
(interaction fraction in [0, 1], |mean(ST − S1)| ≤ 1).

The effect on the headline is large and in the direction the audit predicted:

| | before | after |
|---|---:|---:|
| quotable analyses | 24 of 42 | **8 quotable of 56** |
| mean interaction fraction, quotable | 0.375 | **0.148** |
| mean interaction fraction, ungated | 0.475 | 0.569 |

The denominator moved from 42 to 56 because the Sobol campaign now covers eight
models rather than six: `mainen_l5pc` and `migliore_ca1` were added under the
`probe-2` regeneration. The "before" column therefore describes the old rule on
the old panel and is not a like-for-like comparison of the gate alone.

Of the 48 analyses now excluded, 8 fail completeness alone, 16 fail precision
alone, 2 fail plausibility alone, and 21 fail more than one criterion. The eight
that survive come from five models: `pospischil_fs` (3), `pospischil_rs` (2),
`hh_point`, `hh_cable` and `mainen_l5pc` (1 each). Seven of the eight come from
the four models with the largest base N (256); `khaliq_purkinje` (N = 128),
`bbp_l5pc` (N = 64) and `migliore_ca1` (N = 32) contribute none.

Base N is not the whole story, and the exception is worth stating rather than
smoothing over: `mainen_l5pc` has the joint-smallest base N in the panel (32)
and still yields one quotable analysis. The precision gate compares the widest
S1 confidence half-width against the largest S1 it describes, so a model with a
single large, clean first-order effect can clear it at small N while a model
with diffuse effects fails it at large N. Reading the survivors as simply "the
big-N models" is wrong in one case of eight.

**This is not a finding that interactions are weak.** It is a finding that at
these base sample sizes the Sobol estimates mostly cannot support a
quantitative statement in either direction, which is why the phrase
"interactions are pervasive" no longer appears without a precision
qualification. Doubling N with independent scrambles until the estimates and
confidence widths stabilise is the fix, and it is not in this release.

**Three of the six Sobol campaigns were recomputed** (§5.4). `bbp_l5pc` moved
most, 0.482 → **0.538**; `khaliq_purkinje` and `hh_point` moved by less than
0.005. The three campaigns that were verified rather than re-run reproduced
their previous interaction fractions to three decimal places, which is an
independent check that the verification was sound.

**The claim that "spike-frequency adaptation is ~95% interaction-driven" is
withdrawn.** That feature has by far the *lowest* valid fraction (0.53 — nearly
half its design mean-imputed) and by far the *highest* apparent interaction, which
is exactly the bias direction the imputation predicts.

### 12.0 The primary decision, at lineage level

The decision table is evaluated on **7 implementations representing 5 source
lineages** — the primary cohort, which is **N3-passing with every
N4-unidentifiable parameter projected out of the sampled space before ensembles
are built** (§5.0.3). It is not "gate-passing", and no cohort in this project is.
Every criterion that used to count model ids counts lineages.

#### How this tally may and may not be read

The preregistered algorithm returns a label. What that label *means* depends on
how strong its inputs are, and the 2026-08-09 post-response-2 audit was right
that `5/5` invites a reading the evidence does not carry. Four points, all of
them now emitted with the decision in `decision.json`
(`criterion_dependence`, `interpretation`):

1. **The five H-G criteria are not five independent tests.** Four are computed
   from the same endpoint table and four from the same `synergy_area` endpoint,
   over the same five lineages. Two of those four are sign agreements on the same
   quantity under different conditioning. They cannot fail independently, so the
   count overstates the number of distinct confirmations.
2. **Both direction criteria are 4/5 lineages**, whose exact two-sided sign
   probability under a fair null is **p = 0.375**. That is a majority, not a
   significant one; with five lineages no sign test on this panel can reach
   p < 0.05.
3. **The unique-R² ratio is a lower bound, not a point estimate.** The identity
   contribution is floored at 1e-4, so the >1,651× figure is an artefact of a
   near-zero denominator — and the denominator is the finding: identity
   explains 8.8% of the variance alone, all of it shared with sampling and
   geometry, so it is collinear with them rather than irrelevant. The
   interpretable quantity is the **size-matched ratio, 14.4 with a wide
   interval [2.8, 89.7]** (§10), and that is what should be quoted.
4. **The transfer criterion is a median over five folds that hides a severely
   negative one**, and the classifier clears its baseline by about twelve points
   over five folds. Both are now reported as fold distributions — every fold, the
   worst fold, the IQR and how many beat the intercept-only null — because the
   median alone concealed the spread.

So the honest formulation is: **the preregistered decision algorithm returns H-G
on this arm, as an exploratory decision-framework outcome.** The statement the
data support is that *sampling, conditioning, covariance and model geometry
materially influence the sign and magnitude of measured higher-order interactions
across the tested models*. The data do **not** establish that the interaction
structure is generally non-biological, nor that biologically meaningful parameter
modules are absent. **H-B is unresolved** — see §12.0.1.

| criterion | supports | value | passed |
|---|---|---:|---|
| conditioning effect direction agreement across LINEAGES | H-G | 0.8000 | yes |
| high-covariance subset effect direction agreement (lineages) | H-G | 0.8000 | yes |
| unique R2(sampling+geometry) / unique R2(model identity) [synergy_area] | H-G | 1651.4029 | yes |
| out-of-sample R2 for a held-out LINEAGE (sampling+geometry) | H-G | 0.4988 | yes |
| sampling regime predictable across unseen lineages | H-G | 0.2926 | yes |
| statistical synergy modules validated by direct perturbation | H-B | 0.1667 | no |
| top-synergy tuples more non-additive than matched controls | H-B | 0.9868 | no |

**H-G: 5/5. H-B: 0/2. No overall label is asserted.**

**The two H-B criteria are evaluable again, and both fail.** Under §0.0.1 they
were withheld and the tally read `0/0`: the stored perturbation battery recorded
no measurement schema, and its backgrounds and candidate tuples were derived
from feature rows that the probe-3 regeneration had replaced beneath it, so
`run_crossmodel` refused to score from it. The probe-4 regeneration rebuilt the
battery and its half-step twin from the current corpus with a written identity
(§0.0.2), so the criteria are computed from inputs that provably belong to the
rows they claim to test, and the tally is `0/2`.

What they now say, on the three repeatable lineages the numerical precondition
admits (`khaliq_purkinje`, `migliore_ca1`, `pospischil_rs`): functional support
**0.167 for top-synergy tuples against 0.167 for matched middle-Ω controls** —
equal — and a non-additivity ratio of **0.99×**, paired difference −0.002 SD
with a lineage-clustered 95% CI of [−0.023, +0.037]. On that subset the
statistically synergistic tuples are indistinguishable from their controls.

**This is a failure to demonstrate H-B, not a demonstration that H-B is false**,
and the label remains UNRESOLVED. Three lineages is the minimum the precondition
accepts; the interval is wide; and the same 18 top-synergy tuples were selected
under both corpora, so nothing here says the selection is unstable. What changed
is that the answer can be read at all.

**The previous scoring rule still stands and is why the battery matters.** They are computed from the same
non-additivity η, and until the 2026-08-09 audit nothing required that η to
survive a refinement of the time step before it was scored — even though
`perturbation_inference.py` had already measured that it largely does not. The
precondition now applied is: every model in the battery must have half-step
results; only repeatable implementations may be scored; and below three
repeatable lineages the criteria are *not evaluable* rather than failed.

**These results cannot distinguish weak biological enrichment from no
enrichment.** The statement used throughout this document is:

> Current data do not provide numerically repeatable, adequately powered evidence
> for H-B. They do not demonstrate that H-B is absent.

Three further reasons not to read the H-B side as a negative finding. The
precondition itself is post hoc (§12.2). The three-lineage minimum is exactly the
number that survived, which is a coincidence worth naming rather than glossing
over. And the *random*-control contrast on the same three lineages (3.25×,
interval excluding zero) would have passed a test of the same form; the matched
control is the harder and more appropriate comparator, but a reader should know
that the choice of comparator, not the data, is what separates a pass from a fail
here.

Half-step coverage was completed for `bbp_l5pc` and `migliore_ca1`, which had
none. Of the seven implementations in the battery, **three are numerically
repeatable** — `hh_cable`, `migliore_ca1`, `pospischil_rs` — spanning three
lineages, exactly the minimum, so the criteria are evaluable. On that subset:

* functional support **0.444** against 0.167 for controls, below the 0.5 bar;
* the top-versus-matched contrast is **1.91×**, paired difference +0.182 SD with
  a lineage-clustered 95% CI of **[−0.020, +0.530]** — which includes zero.

**Read that as a failure to demonstrate, not a demonstration of absence.** The
interval is consistent with no enrichment *and* with enrichment of half a
standard deviation, because it rests on three lineages. The random-control
contrast on the same subset is 3.25× with CI [+0.022, …], i.e. it would have
passed; it is not scored, because it is the same measurement with a different
denominator (§12.1). What the table now says is that the causal-module claim is
**not supported by evidence that survives its own numerical check** — not that
the modules have been shown to be spurious.

**The H-G label is produced by H-B failing, and that is a weaker thing than it
sounds.** The decision rule returns H-G when the generic side passes and the
biological side does not; it does not distinguish "the biological criteria were
tested on an adequate sample and failed" from "they were tested on three
lineages and could not resolve anything". This case is the second. Nothing about
H-G's own five criteria got stronger — they are the same four narrow passes and
one lower bound described below. The audit's formulation is more accurate than
the label: sampling and conditioning materially influence the estimated
interaction structure; cross-lineage sign generalisation is encouraging but not
confirmatory; and whether statistically selected tuples are numerically robust
causal modules is **unresolved**.

The precondition is **not preregistered**; it was written after the
repeatability outcome was known. See §12.2 and `preregistration.md` Addendum 4.

**The four H-G criteria that remain, read honestly.** Conditioning moves the
endpoint toward synergy in 4 of 5 lineages, exact two-sided sign p = 0.375 —
and 0.0625 is the *smallest* p a five-unit panel can produce, so no
configuration of these lineages could have reached significance. The
high-covariance contrast is likewise 4/5 at p = 0.375. The lineage-transfer
median of **0.499** conceals a `hodgkin_huxley_1952` fold at **−72.3**: on that
held-out lineage the model predicts far worse than the training mean. The
classifier clears its 0.172 baseline with **0.293**, over 11 classes.

**The unique-R² ratio needs its denominator read, not its magnitude.** It is
**>1,651×**, and the reason is that unique R²(model identity) is **0.000** — it
sits below the 1e-4 resolution floor, so the ratio is a lower bound produced by
dividing by a floor, not a measurement. What that zero means matters more than
the ratio: model identity explains **8.8%** of `synergy_area` variance *on its
own* (`r2_alone` 0.088), and **all** of it is shared with sampling and geometry
(`r2_shared` 0.088, `r2_unique` 0.000). Identity is therefore not irrelevant —
it is **collinear** with the sampling and geometry axes in this design, and a
commonality analysis cannot separate them. Reporting the ratio without that
sentence invites the reading that model identity does not matter, which these
data do not support. The bounded measurement is the size-matched cluster
bootstrap: **14.4×, CI [2.8, 89.7]** over 5 lineages, and that is the number
this section rests on.

Their weight comes from agreeing in direction across independent contrasts, not
from any individual margin.

**Variance components.** The flat adjusted ICC of model identity is 0.262 and
the variance-partition coefficient 0.055; both are **descriptive only**, since
the fixed covariates conditioned on are themselves the H-G hypothesis. The
nested model separates the levels and **both now converge** under REML:
implementation ICC **0.262** over 7 groups, lineage ICC **0.172** over 5 groups,
each marked `quotable: true` by `nested_variance_components`.

Two corrections of record attach to this paragraph. An early version gave
lineage ICC 0.166 and implementation ICC 0.152 and stated that both converged;
those values were stale and the convergence claim was false. The next version
correctly reported that the **lineage level did not converge** on 5 groups and
withheld the number. It converges on the probe-3 rows, so the number is quoted
again — but it is quoted because the fit changed, not because the standard did.
The convergence flag is read from the artifact, never asserted in prose.

**Sobol** contributes 8 quotable analyses of 55 completed (of 56 possible) under
the precision-gated rule, with mean interaction fraction 0.148 (§11.2).

**The sensitivity cascade has 9 declared arms but only 6 substantively distinct
ones.** `retain_partial_coverage` and `discovery_only` select exactly the same

The one analytic point from that table that survives its deletion, because it is
about the criterion rather than about a superseded number:

**The direction-agreement criterion is contaminated by tiny accepted cells.** It
averages Δ net O-information across *all* tolerances, giving a 66-sample
very-strict ensemble the same weight as a 6,642-sample permissive one. A model
whose mean turns positive on the strength of its very-strict value alone is
recording a fluctuation that §6.1 shows is about 2σ, not a dissent. At the
permissive tolerance alone, agreement has been unanimous in every version of
this analysis. The criterion is left exactly as preregistered — rescoring it now,
having seen which value moved it, is the failure mode described in §12.1 — but a
disagreeing unit should be read as "disagrees after averaging in measurements
that cannot be made", not as a neuron behaving differently.

### 12.1 The tally is unstable under changes to the criterion set

The previous release reported **H-B 4/6** and reached the same conclusion. The
denominator changed because the intraclass-correlation criterion was withdrawn
(§11). It was a criterion H-B *failed*, so removing it moved H-B's pass rate
from 0.67 to 0.80 without a single number about neurons changing.

This is the second time the arithmetic has moved for procedural rather than
empirical reasons. The first was in the other direction: the random-control
criterion now in row 5 was **added after preregistration**, once the corrected
perturbation battery had been run and its result was known. That addition raised
both the H-B denominator and its pass count.

Both changes were individually defensible — a wrongly computed statistic should
not vote, and a stronger control is a better test. Taken together they show that
the vote-counting procedure is not a measurement. Its output depends on how many
criteria each hypothesis is given and on decisions made after the data were
seen, and a rule with 5 criteria per side has no resolution finer than 0.2.

**The verdict label should therefore be read as a summary of the table, not as a
result in its own right.** What is load-bearing is the individual rows and their
effect sizes.

An earlier version of this paragraph said H-G's five criteria "pass by wide
margins and would survive any reasonable reweighting". The 2026-08-09 audit
contradicted that directly, and it was right. Three of the five pass narrowly:

* both direction-agreement criteria are **4/5 lineages, exact two-sided sign
  p = 0.375** — the smallest p an outcome this size can produce with five units
  is 0.0625, so no configuration of five lineages could have reached
  significance;
* the regime classifier clears its threshold at **0.293 against a 0.172
  baseline** over 11 classes, a margin small relative to its fold-to-fold
  variability; and
* the lineage-transfer median of **0.499** conceals a `hodgkin_huxley_1952`
  fold at **−72.3** — transfer is heterogeneous and fails catastrophically for
  one lineage, predicting far worse than the training mean.

The fourth, the unique-R² ratio, is a **lower bound** created by identity
variance falling below a numerical floor, not a point estimate of >1,651×. Its
denominator is the finding: unique R²(model identity) is 0.000 while identity
explains 8.8% of the variance *alone*, all of it shared with sampling and
geometry — identity is collinear with those axes in this design, not
irrelevant. Only the size-matched version (**14.4×, CI [2.8, 89.7]**) is a
bounded measurement.

The honest statement is that the H-G rows agree in *direction* across every
version of this analysis while being individually weak, and that their weight
comes from consistency across independent contrasts rather than from any one
margin.

Blueprint §19.3 defines H-M as requiring that

> synergy or redundancy signs are largely governed by sampling regime **but**
> stable, *functionally validated* module identities differ systematically
> across neuronal classes.

The first clause holds: the sign is regime-governed (5/5, with 8/8 direction
agreement at the permissive tolerance on two independent contrasts, and the
effect exceeding conditional bootstrap noise by 4-5x in the five implementations with adequate samples, §6.0). The
modules are stable across estimators (per-model Jaccard 0.82, minimum 0.67).

**The second clause — *functional validation* — is not met on the evidence, and
until the 2026-08-09 audit the decision table's two H-B rows overstated it.**
Both are now withheld as not evaluable (§12.0). Four things have to be read
together:

* Its **precision is limited by background variability**: the per-tuple
  bootstrap intervals over the eight backgrounds mostly include zero (§11.1).
  The earlier "below the measurement floor in four of eight models" claim is
  withdrawn — it was invalid — but the imprecision it was gesturing at is real
  and is now measured properly.
* It is **absent for waveform readouts** on the same simulations (§11), so
  whatever is being validated is a property of a spike-timing measurement rather
  than of the parameters alone.
* Since the 2026-08-09 audit it is **gated on its own numerical repeatability**,
  which it never was. Both perturbation criteria are computed from the same η,
  and η at the campaign time step had never been required to survive refinement
  before being scored (§11.1.1, §12.0).

A criterion that clears a threshold on a panel mean, while being imprecise at
the level of individual tuples, dependent on which quantity is measured, and
resting on non-additivity that has to pass a separate numerical test before it
means anything, does not establish that statistical modules are functionally
real.

An earlier version of this list opened with "the ratios that pass (1.87× and
2.77×) do so against a fixed 1.5× threshold, but neither paired test is
significant (p = 0.195 and p = 0.078) and the matched-control interval starts at
1.03". Every number in that sentence was superseded by the regenerated battery
and it should have been removed then; the current values are in §11 and §12.0.

The first release argued for overriding the rule toward H-G on the grounds that
"statistical modules frequently fail direct perturbation validation". **That
specific claim was wrong** — it rested on the perturbation design faults
documented in §11, and the corrected battery does show enrichment in the right
direction. But the replacement claim has now been revised downward three times,
and on the current evidence the honest position is that functional validation is
**unresolved**, not established. The decision table no longer returns H-M; it
returns "H-B not evaluable", which is the same statement in the framework's own
vocabulary.

### 12.2 The numerical precondition is post hoc, and we are not hiding it

The rule that withholds both H-B criteria (§12.0) was written on 9 August 2026,
in response to the independent audit, **with the 2/5 repeatability outcome
already known**. A rule added after seeing the data it will act on cannot carry
the weight of one fixed in advance, and §12.1 is about exactly this failure
mode, so the qualification belongs here rather than in a footnote.

Three things bound how much room it leaves:

* The **repeatability rule it consumes was prespecified.** Median |Δη| ≤ 0.05
  feature SD *and* ≥ 80% of tuple means keeping their sign was fixed in
  `tools/perturbation_inference.py` before the half-step battery was run, and is
  used here unchanged. The threshold was not chosen to produce this answer.
* The only genuinely new number is the **minimum lineage count**, set to 3. Its
  justification is a property of the estimator, not of the outcome: the
  criterion is scored on a lineage-clustered bootstrap interval, and an interval
  over two clusters is a statement about two numbers. Below 3 the criterion
  would be scored on something that cannot be estimated; above 3 it would be
  unreachable for this panel.
* The rule **withholds our own only passing H-B criterion.** It moves the result
  away from a positive claim, not toward one.

`decision.json` records `perturbation_precondition.preregistered: false` and its
provenance, so the distinction survives being read out of the artifact rather
than out of this document. `preregistration.md` Addendum 4 records it alongside
the other post-hoc changes of this round, including one — the non-enforcement of
the registered N4 inclusion gate for `tomko_ca1` — that is a departure from a
registered rule rather than an addition to one.

## 13. What this means for the motivating question

The **sign** of higher-order structure — whether an ensemble looks synergistic or
redundant — is a property of how the ensemble was built, not of which neuron it
came from. It appears in a gene circuit with no neurons in it; it is set
identically across models spanning 1 to 2,304 compartments; sampling and geometry
explain ~14.4× more unique endpoint variance than model identity even after size
matching; and a shuffled null reproduces 45% of the naive effect.

The **identity** of the parameters involved is not arbitrary. The extreme tuples
are reproducible across estimators (per-model Jaccard 0.83) and model identity
is decodable from information summaries at 0.590 against a 0.151 baseline —
both descriptive only, for the reasons given in §12.0. Those tuples are also
2.5–3.8× more non-additive under direct perturbation than matched or random
controls, but neither paired test reaches significance at the lineage level
(p = 0.188 and 0.063), and on the three implementations whose non-additivity is
numerically repeatable the matched contrast falls to 1.91× with an interval that
includes zero. The preregistered decision therefore returns **H-G**, not H-M.

Interpreting the *sign* of O-information as a biological property remains
unsupported. Interpreting *which parameters* appear in extreme tuples as
mechanistically meaningful is **unresolved** — not established, and not refuted
either. Five qualifications belong in the same sentence as the claim, not in a
footnote:

1. The support is **readout-specific**. Pooled enrichment over matched controls
   is 2.79× for first ISI and 2.97× for firing rate, but 1.32× for AP amplitude
   and **0.85× for AP half-width** — measured on the same simulations, same
   tuples, same perturbations (§11). "These parameters form a module" is not a
   complete statement; the complete statement names the readout.
2. The support is **imprecise per tuple**. Bootstrap intervals over the eight
   backgrounds mostly include zero: 16 of 42 top tuples exclude it, and 10 of
   those 16 come from the two HH implementations, which are one lineage (§11.1).
   The earlier claim that four of eight models were "unresolvable" against a
   spike-count floor is withdrawn as invalid, not merely restated.
3. It is **not significant at the inferential unit**: p = 0.188 against matched
   controls and 0.063 against random ones over five lineages. An earlier version
   of this paragraph quoted p = 0.195 and 0.078, and a later one quoted 0.047
   and 0.016 over seven implementations and called them significant. The first
   pair was superseded by the regenerated battery; the second counted
   implementations as independent units and is withdrawn.
4. Since the 2026-08-09 audit it is **conditional on a numerical repeatability
   check** the non-additivity had never been required to pass — and on the
   3 of 7 implementations that pass it, the matched contrast is 1.91× with a
   lineage-clustered interval of [−0.020, +0.530] and **does not clear zero**
   (§11.1.1, §12.0). Three lineages cannot distinguish no enrichment from
   substantial enrichment, so this is a failure to demonstrate rather than a
   demonstration of absence.
5. The conditioning dose-response is monotone wherever it is resolvable, but
   **resolvable coverage collapses 5 → 6 → 2 → 1** across tolerances, so the
   panel-level curve is not estimable (§6.1).

None of this is about real neurons, which this project cannot address. What it
is about is the construction: an ensemble study of this kind measures the joint
behaviour of a prior, a conditioning map, an admissibility rule, and a readout —
and the last of those has turned out to matter as much as any of the others.

## 14. Methodological recommendations

1. **Report a matched-search surrogate control.** Without one, ~45% of tuples
   look "synergy-dominated" in an ensemble with no dependence at all.
2. **Report the unconditioned ensemble.** The conditioned-vs-unconditioned
   contrast is the interpretable quantity.
3. **Do not read covariance magnitude as redundancy.** Two ensembles here at
   mean |r| ≈ 0.28 sit on opposite sides of zero.
4. **Draw perturbation backgrounds from the ensemble you are testing**, keep
   displacements inside the declared prior, and normalise on that population.
   Getting any of these wrong turned a real 2–4× enrichment into a null.
5. **Never mean-impute phenotype-dependent missing outputs inside a Saltelli
   design.** It biases interaction fractions upward, and the worst-affected
   feature will look like the most interactive one.
6. **Audit numerical convergence on prior samples and on every analysed
   feature**, not on spike count at the canonical point. Four of eight models
   here passed the narrow check and failed the proper one.
6a. **Refine the reference in every dimension you claim to have converged.** The
   first Mainen audit compared a fine time step against a reference at the *same
   mesh*, so it could not see spatial error and "the refined mesh reproduces all
   features" was never tested (§5.1.1).
6b. **Never set a convergence tolerance finer than the readout's quantum.** A
   spike count over a fixed window cannot resolve below one spike; a tolerance
   below that is unsatisfiable at any mesh and turns threshold-adjacent spikes
   into permanent convergence failures.
7. **Bootstrap over independent sources, not over rows and not over
   implementations**, when endpoints are derived from a shared campaign. Two
   things have to be right and only the first is obvious: the resampling *unit*
   must be the independent source, and the statistic must **weight each source
   equally**. Resampling lineages while pooling their implementations' rows
   still gives a two-implementation lineage double weight, and it cost this
   project a published significance claim (§11).
8. **Name the readout in the claim.** "Parameters X, Y, Z form a functional
   module" is incomplete. On identical simulations and identical perturbations,
   r(Ω, |η|) is −0.45 for a spike-count readout and −0.00 for a waveform
   readout (§11). A module claim that does not say which quantity was measured
   is not falsifiable, and pooling readouts produces an average (−0.22) that
   describes none of them.
9. **Compare every effect with the uncertainty of the estimator that produced
   it, not with the quantum of a single observation.** A spike count over a
   400 ms window is quantised at 2.5 Hz, but the reported statistic is a mean
   over eight backgrounds, whose grid is 0.3125 Hz, and a deterministic grid is
   not a detection limit in any case. This project got that wrong once and drew
   four conclusions from it (§11.1). Report the standard error over the units
   actually averaged, and test repeatability by re-running at a finer time
   step.
10. **Do not let a vote-counting rule stand in for a result.** Adding one
    criterion after seeing the data, and removing one that was computed wrongly,
    each moved this project's tally without changing any measurement (§12.1).
    Report the criteria and their effect sizes; treat the label as a summary.
11. **Make a criterion consume its own validity check.** This project measured
    that its perturbation non-additivity mostly does not survive halving the
    time step, wrote the result to a file, and went on scoring the criterion
    built from that non-additivity for two revisions, because nothing read the
    file (§12.0). A validity check that no consumer is required to read is
    documentation, not a gate.
12. **Distinguish "not evaluable" from "failed" in the decision itself.** A
    criterion that could not be tested is not evidence against the hypothesis it
    was written to test. Scoring it zero and scoring it absent lead to opposite
    conclusions from the same ignorance.
13. **Replay the waveform, not only the feature row.** Deterministic feature
    replay proves that a computation is reproducible; a trace that is wrong the
    same way twice reproduces perfectly. A stratified sample of retained
    voltage, checked for range, baseline stability, spike shape, block,
    discontinuity and finiteness, found two defect classes here that 3,339
    exact feature replays could not (§5.5).

### 14.1 The experiment this project should run next

The finding in §11 is a *confound*, not yet a result about representation. Two
explanations fit it equally well:

* **Readout representation.** Spike-timing and waveform readouts are sensitive
  to different parameters, so different tuples are modules for each.
* **Counting nonlinearity.** Rate and first-ISI are threshold-crossing counts;
  crossing a threshold is itself a non-additive operation, so *any* tuple would
  look more non-additive in a counted readout regardless of representation.

These are separable with no new simulation design, only new analysis of a
rerun battery: repeat tuple selection and perturbation under **two disjoint
conditioning vectors** (waveform-shape features versus rate features), and add a
**graded, non-counted rate readout** — mean inverse-ISI, or a spike-time-based
rate — as a third arm. If representation is doing the work, the graded rate
readout behaves like the counted one. If counting is doing the work, it behaves
like the waveform readouts. Either outcome is publishable and the current design
cannot distinguish them.

### 14.2 What a confirmatory panel would require

The 2026-08-09 audit answered "are more models needed?" with a distinction worth
adopting verbatim. **For the present exploratory conclusion, no**: adding models
repairs none of the open problems — N2's partial coverage, the N4 failures,
perturbation repeatability, pipeline ordering, documentation drift — and those
should be settled first. Increasing samples *within* existing models would sharpen the
estimators without adding a single independent lineage, which is the quantity
that actually bounds the claim.

**For a confirmatory cross-neuron or class-general claim, yes.** The headline
runs on 7 implementations over 5 lineages; enforcing N4 (`n4_strict`) takes the
discovery cohort to 6 implementations over 4 lineages. Its planning targets:

* add **4–6 genuinely independent, gate-passing source lineages**;
* reach roughly **12–16 valid lineages overall**; and
* require **≥3 independent lineages per neuron class** before any
  class-specific claim.

These are design targets, not a power calculation; a lineage-level
simulation-based power analysis should fix the final number. Its inclusion
procedure, which this project would follow rather than improvise:

1. register lineage, implementation, class, accession, license, protocol
   coverage and expected mechanisms **before** looking at any scientific outcome;
2. use a new immutable campaign namespace — do not extend the frozen discovery
   campaign;
3. run N1–N7 outcome-blind, **including a genuine N2 source reproduction**,
   before inclusion;
4. reject or remediate models with inactive parameters *before* their dimensions
   enter the geometry — which is precisely what was not done for `bbp_l5pc` and
   `tomko_ca1` here;
5. size campaigns from an accepted-ensemble precision target (≥256–512 accepted
   samples in important cells), not from raw simulation count;
6. keep the current discovery result frozen and treat new lineages as
   replication, assessing heterogeneity before pooling;
7. aggregate implementations within lineage and give each lineage equal weight;
8. re-run estimator reliability, E7, matched, perturbation and Sobol under the
   new cohort; and
9. record gates, revision, environment, seeds, raw logs and artifact hashes in
   one immutable manifest.

Two design changes this project's own retained-waveform battery argues for
(§5.5): set the **hyperpolarising amplitude per model from measured input
resistance** rather than as a fixed absolute current, and set the
**pre-stimulus settling period per model from a measured settling time** rather
than a fixed delay.

## 15. Limitations

These bound what the results above can support. They are declared in
[`preregistration.md`](preregistration.md) §12, not discovered afterwards.

1. **Screening scale, not confirmatory scale.** The blueprint envisages ~10⁷
   simulations on a cluster; this ran ~10⁵ on one 14-core workstation. Sample
   sizes are unequal across models (8,192 for cheap point models down to 512 for
   `migliore_ca1`), so every cross-model endpoint is also computed on a
   size-matched subsample.
2. **No tier A or B priors.** Every prior is a published optimisation range
   (tier C, `bbp_l5pc` only) or an expert-defined range (tier D). Nothing here
   measures biological variability, and the width of a tier-D prior is a choice,
   not a measurement.
3. **The conditioning target is each model's own canonical phenotype**, with
   dispersion taken from its unconditioned ensemble — not a measured cell-level
   feature distribution. Ensemble regime E5 (per-cell conditioning) is therefore
   not executed.
4. **Gaussian estimator blindness.** Finding E1 above: the primary estimator
   cannot see product-type synergy. Neuronal conclusions drawn from it are
   conclusions about second-order-visible structure only.
5. **`migliore_ca1` has the smallest sample size in the panel** (256 E1
   samples), so its per-model estimates are the least precise and it is the one
   family that leave-one-out transfer fails for. Its *solver* settings are no
   longer a limitation: it runs at dt = 0.025 ms on the 1,106-segment
   d_lambda(100 Hz) mesh, which is exactly `dt0.025_mesh1.0x` — the setting its
   convergence audit accepts with full agreement on all 13 conditioning
   features. An earlier version of this list said it ran "one step coarser than
   its convergence audit accepts" with "a small systematic bias" in absolute
   rate features. That was true before the corrective rerun and was never
   updated; it is withdrawn.
6. **`khaliq_purkinje` has no rheobase-normalised protocol family** because it
   fires spontaneously. The normalised-protocol analysis covers 7 of 8 models.
7. **Sobol now covers all eight screening models**, including `migliore_ca1` and
   `mainen_l5pc`, which were added when the campaign was regenerated under
   `probe-2`; the panel is 8 models × 7 features = 56 analyses. It is their
   *quotability* that is limited, not their execution: 8 of the 56 clear the
   completeness, precision and plausibility gate, and `migliore_ca1` contributes
   none of the eight. An earlier version of this list said Sobol was not run for
   Migliore at all, which stopped being true after the regeneration.
8. **E6 is a clustered *prior*, not a clustered accepted ensemble.** Its basins
   are drawn from the prior rather than from accepted solutions; E7 provides the
   conditioned high-covariance contrast.
9. **One morphology per model.** Morphology swaps within a model family
   (blueprint §13.3) are represented only by the `hh_point` / `hh_cable` pair.
10. **Functional module validation is readout-bound and numerically
    unresolved.** The enrichment holds for spike-timing readouts and vanishes
    for waveform readouts (§11), and most individual tuples have bootstrap
    intervals over backgrounds that include zero (§11.1). Whether the readout
    dependence reflects representation or the nonlinearity of counting is **not
    decidable from this design**; §14.1 states the experiment that would decide
    it. Since the 2026-08-09 audit the perturbation criteria are additionally
    gated on their own half-step repeatability check, which they had never been
    (§11.1.1, §12.0). (An earlier version of this point invoked a spike-count
    "measurement floor"; that argument was invalid and is withdrawn. An earlier
    version also quoted paired p = 0.195 and 0.078, which were superseded by the
    regenerated battery.)
14. **Five sampled parameters are not identifiable through the frozen
    protocol**, in two models that are *not* excluded: `bbp_l5pc` (primary
    cohort) and `tomko_ca1` (replication). They are still drawn and still vary,
    so they contribute unconstrained dimensions to the geometry that
    O-information and intrinsic dimension are computed on. The primary cohort is
    therefore **N3-passing, not gate-passing**, and the consequence is measured
    by the `n4_strict` and `n4_retained` arms rather than assumed away (§5.0.3).
15. **Two trace-level defects survive the campaign's QC thresholds.** A
    stratified retention of 445 waveforms found sub-physiological
    hyperpolarisation in 2 eligible traces (0.45%) that fall in the 10 mV gap
    between the QC bound of −160 mV and a −150 mV plausibility band, and slow
    baseline settling drift in 31 traces (7.0%) that the ripple-based
    `unstable_baseline` flag cannot see. Both affect features measured against
    the pre-stimulus baseline; neither is repaired, because both thresholds are
    frozen campaign properties (§5.5).
11. **The conditioning dose-response is not estimable.** Tolerance coverage
    falls 8 → 8 → 6 → 3 models and *resolvable* coverage falls 5 → 6 → 2 → 1
    (§6.1). The direction of the effect is established, with conditional bootstrap SNR 4-5
    in five models; its dependence on tolerance is not estimable at panel level.
13. **No minimum-sample policy for entropy endpoints.** Sobol gates on
    `VALID_FRACTION_FLOOR`; the conditioning path has no equivalent, so a
    66-sample ensemble carries the same weight as a 6,642-sample one in every
    panel mean and in the preregistered direction-agreement criterion (§12).
12. **`mainen_l5pc` is not spatially converged.** Against a reference refined in
    both time and space, its production setting fails one of the 13 conditioning
    features — `adaptation_index` — on one of seven prior samples, through a
    single threshold-adjacent spike (§5.1.1). Its accepted ensemble is therefore
    defined partly by a quantity that is not mesh-converged for it. Refining
    further would roughly double the cost of the panel's most expensive model
    and would not remove threshold-adjacent behaviour at any finite mesh.

## 16. Reproducing this

One command rebuilds everything, in dependency order, with per-stage logs:

```bash
./tools/run_all.sh
```

The stage list is `assets mechanisms calibrate convergence canonical campaign
sobol analysis matched perturb crossmodel robustness benchmarks reliability
gates waveforms numerical notebook report inventory`. Two orderings in it are
load-bearing and are asserted by
`tests/unit/test_audit_regressions.py`: **`perturb` precedes `crossmodel`**,
because the cross-model decision scores two H-B criteria from the perturbation
artifacts and reads their repeatability verdict; and the **`numerical` and
`waveforms` stages are soft**, because this release is known to fail N1 and N4
and to carry unresolved waveform findings, and a hard failure there would abort
the pipeline before the report and the release manifest whose whole job is to
describe that failure.

The individual steps, if you want them separately:

```bash
pip install -e .
python tools/fetch_modeldb_index.py
python tools/fetch_models.py
python tools/build_mechanisms.py
python tools/numerical_qc.py --spatial          # first-pass QC (historical record)
python tools/convergence_audit.py mainen_l5pc   # per-model, per-feature convergence
python tools/verify_convergence_evidence.py     # check the stored evidence, do not re-run it
python tools/canonical_audit.py                 # manifests, under CAMPAIGN_SOLVER
python tools/run_campaign.py --experiment screening
python tools/build_notebook.py
jupyter nbconvert --execute --to notebook --inplace notebooks/COMPASS_results.ipynb
pytest
```

Two provenance rules are enforced rather than documented, because both were
broken in earlier releases and neither broke anything visible at the time.

**One solver contract.** `CAMPAIGN_SOLVER` is the single source of settings.
`tools/canonical_audit.py` reads it rather than keeping its own, and
`tests/unit/test_solver_contract.py` compares the *stored manifests* — not the
code that writes them — against it and against the mesh the adapters build. The
first-pass `numerical_qc.json` is retained as history and is not consulted for
production settings (D24).

**Cached simulations carry their identity.** Every Sobol shard has a sidecar
`S_XXXXXX.identity.json` recording the solver contract, the design and a
simulation-scoped code hash. A shard whose identity does not match is moved to
`_superseded/<timestamp>/` and re-run — never deleted, and never silently
reused. Stored shards from before stamping are not back-dated: run
`tools/run_sobol.py --verify-stored` to reproduce one real block per model and
stamp only what matches bit-for-bit (D25, D26).

Every result table carries the experiment configuration hash
(`configs/experiments/screening.json`), the random seed, the compiled mechanism
hash and the solver settings that produced it.

## 17. Scope of the claims

| level | claim | status |
|---|---|---|
| **Observed** | the metrics reported above take these values in these declared ensembles | established |
| **Statistical** | these ensembles are synergy- or redundancy-dominated under the stated estimators | established |
| **Functional** | the measured non-additivity η takes these values for the tested tuples | established as a measurement |
| **Functional (inferential)** | statistical synergy *selects* functionally interacting tuples | **not established** — 2.49×/3.83× at the lineage level with p = 0.188/0.063, readout-dependent (0.85× for AP half-width), imprecise per tuple, and now withheld from the decision as not evaluable pending numerical repeatability |
| **Biological** | the same interactions occur in real neurons | **not established, not claimed** |

The functional level is split because the two statements have very different
support. That the perturbations produce the η values reported is a measurement
and stands. That those η values *validate* the tuples statistical synergy picked
out is an inference, and on the current evidence it is **unresolved** rather than
suggestive: the comparison is not significant at the inferential unit, and the
η it rests on has not passed the half-step repeatability check that now gates it
(§11, §11.1, §12.0). An earlier version of this row quoted 1.87×/2.77× with
p = 0.195/0.078, and a later one 2.97×/4.74× with p = 0.047/0.016 described as
significant; the first pair was superseded by the regenerated battery and the
second counted implementations as independent units.

The last line requires paired molecular and physiological measurements that no
computational study of this kind can supply.
